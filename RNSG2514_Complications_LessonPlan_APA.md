**Course:** RNSG 2514 Nursing III (guest lectures) · **Program:** Lone Star College–North Harris, Associate Degree Nursing (ADN)
**Audience:** Pre-licensure RN students, third semester
**Framework:** NCSBN Clinical Judgment Measurement Model (NCJMM) + Quality and Safety Education for Nurses (QSEN)
**Format:** 4 sessions × 140 minutes (11:00 AM–1:20 PM); 2 Wednesdays + 2 Thursdays · Week 3 Wednesday = Exam (no proctoring required)
**Coordinator/resource person:** Rajrani Sharma, PhD, MSN/Ed, RN

> **Source policy.** Every citation is a federal agency publication, a professional-society clinical guideline, a peer-reviewed journal article, a National Academies consensus report, or a published nursing textbook. No blogs, no anonymous web content, no commercial test-prep material. Each reference was verified against the publisher of record.

> **How this differs from Nursing I.** Nursing I taught *normal* and *screening*. This unit is **deterioration recognition and emergency response**. The dominant NCJMM operations shift from Recognize Cues toward **Analyze Cues → Prioritize Hypotheses → Take Action**, and nearly every topic has a time-critical component. Teach accordingly: fewer definitions, more "what do you do first, and why."

# DAY 1 (Wednesday, Week 1) — Bleeding Disorders, Pregnancy Loss, and Abortion

**Topics:** early- and late-pregnancy bleeding · spontaneous and induced abortion · DIC · hemorrhage response

### Objectives
1. Differentiate causes of first-, second-, and third-trimester bleeding by presentation.
2. Distinguish placenta previa from abruptio placentae and state the nursing action that differs.
3. Describe types of spontaneous abortion and the management options for early pregnancy loss.
4. Prioritize nursing actions for obstetric hemorrhage, including massive transfusion and DIC recognition.
5. Apply legal and ethical principles to abortion care and pregnancy-loss documentation in Texas.

### 1A. Brief A&P — *Physiologic Integrity*
Placental implantation and the uteroplacental circulation; why the pregnant uterus at term receives roughly 15–20% of cardiac output and can therefore lose a catastrophic volume in minutes; the physiologic hypervolemia of pregnancy that **masks** early hemorrhage — vital signs may remain normal until 15–25% of blood volume is lost.
> *Source:* Lowdermilk et al. (2024).

### 1B. First-trimester bleeding — *Recognize/Analyze Cues*
| Cause | Classic presentation | Priority action |
|---|---|---|
| Threatened abortion | Bleeding, closed cervix, viable IUP | Ultrasound, hCG trend, support |
| Inevitable / incomplete | Bleeding, **open** cervix, cramping ± tissue | Prepare for management; assess bleeding volume |
| Missed abortion | No bleeding or minimal; no fetal cardiac activity | Emotional support; discuss options |
| Complete | Bleeding subsiding, closed cervix, empty uterus | Confirm; RhIG if Rh-negative |
| Recurrent | ≥ 2 consecutive losses | Referral for evaluation |
| **Ectopic pregnancy** | Unilateral pain, amenorrhea, ± bleeding, **shoulder pain**, hypotension | **Emergency** — ruptured EP is hemorrhagic shock |
| Gestational trophoblastic disease | "Grape-like" vesicles, uterus large for dates, hyperemesis, very high hCG | hCG surveillance; **strict contraception during follow-up** |

**Early pregnancy loss management (ACOG PB 200):** three accepted options — **expectant, medical, or surgical (uterine aspiration)**. In patients without medical complications or symptoms requiring urgent evacuation, the plan can safely accommodate **patient preference**. A single preoperative dose of doxycycline is recommended to prevent infection after surgical management. Rh(D) immune globulin should be considered, particularly for later first-trimester losses.
- **Ethics/Legal/Advocacy:** the nurse's role is accurate information, respect for the choice made, and non-abandonment. Know Texas reporting and fetal-remains disposition requirements — verify current statute with the department, as this area changes.
> *Sources:* ACOG (2018d) [PB 200]; ACOG (2020e) [PB 225]; Lowdermilk et al. (2024).

### 1C. Late-pregnancy bleeding — *the single highest-yield contrast in this unit*

| | **Placenta previa** | **Abruptio placentae** |
|---|---|---|
| Bleeding | **Painless**, bright red, often recurrent | Painful, dark red, **may be concealed** |
| Uterus | Soft, relaxed, non-tender | **Rigid, board-like, tender**, hypertonic |
| Onset | Often insidious | Sudden |
| Fetal status | Often reassuring initially | Category II/III common; demise possible |
| Key risk factors | Prior cesarean, prior previa, multiparity, AMA, smoking | Hypertension, trauma, cocaine, smoking, PPROM, prior abruption |
| **Contraindicated** | **Digital vaginal examination — never** | Digital exam also deferred until previa excluded |
| Coagulopathy | Uncommon | **DIC risk is real** |

- **Vasa previa:** painless bleeding with ROM plus **sudden fetal bradycardia/sinusoidal pattern** — the blood is fetal; this is a fetal emergency, not a maternal one.
- **Placenta accreta spectrum:** suspect with previa + prior cesarean; anticipate massive transfusion and possible hysterectomy; requires planned, resourced delivery.

### 1D. Postpartum hemorrhage and DIC — *Emergency/Critical Thinking; Pharmacology; QSEN*
- **Definition (ACOG reVITALize):** cumulative blood loss ≥ 1,000 mL **or** blood loss accompanied by signs/symptoms of hypovolemia within 24 hours of birth, regardless of route. **However**, blood loss > 500 mL after vaginal birth should still be considered abnormal and trigger evaluation.
- **Causes — the 4 Ts:** **T**one (uterine atony, ~70–80%), **T**rauma (lacerations, hematoma, rupture), **T**issue (retained placenta), **T**hrombin (coagulopathy).
- **First nursing action for a boggy uterus:** **fundal massage** — then oxytocin, then escalate.
- **Uterotonics (know contraindications, they are exam gold):**
  | Drug | Caution / contraindication |
  |---|---|
  | Oxytocin | First-line; water intoxication with large volumes |
  | Methylergonovine | **Contraindicated in hypertension/preeclampsia** |
  | Carboprost (15-methyl PGF2α) | **Contraindicated in asthma**; causes diarrhea, fever |
  | Misoprostol | Fever, shivering |
  | Tranexamic acid | Adjunct; give early when indicated |
- **Sequelae of hemorrhage to name:** shock, DIC, acute renal failure, ARDS, loss of fertility, and **Sheehan syndrome** (pituitary necrosis).
- **QI/QSEN:** quantitative blood loss, hemorrhage carts, stage-based protocols, massive transfusion protocol activation, debriefing after every event.
> *Sources:* ACOG (2017b) [PB 183]; Lowdermilk et al. (2024).

### Active learning
- **AL1 — Two-column sort:** 12 clinical findings sorted previa vs abruption, timed, then defended.
- **AL2 — Escalation drill:** boggy uterus + QBL 1,200 mL. Students verbalize actions in order and deliver an **SBAR** call to the provider.

**Exit ticket:** Name the uterotonic contraindicated in preeclampsia and the one contraindicated in asthma.

# DAY 2 (Thursday, Week 1) — Hypertensive Disorders, HELLP, Hyperemesis, Gestational Diabetes

**Topics:** gestational hypertension · preeclampsia with/without severe features · eclampsia · HELLP · hyperemesis gravidarum · gestational diabetes

### Objectives
1. Differentiate the hypertensive disorders of pregnancy using current diagnostic criteria.
2. Recognize preeclampsia with severe features and HELLP syndrome from assessment and laboratory cues.
3. Administer and monitor magnesium sulfate safely, including recognition and treatment of toxicity.
4. Distinguish hyperemesis gravidarum from normal nausea and vomiting of pregnancy.
5. Plan nursing care for gestational diabetes across the antepartum, intrapartum, and immediate postpartum periods.

### 2A. Hypertensive disorders — *Physiologic Integrity; Recognize/Analyze Cues*
- **Scale of the problem:** hypertensive disorders are among the leading causes of maternal and perinatal mortality worldwide; preeclampsia complicates an estimated 2–8% of pregnancies globally, and roughly 16% of maternal deaths in high-income countries are attributable to hypertensive disorders.
- **Classification:**
  | Disorder | Defining features |
  |---|---|
  | Chronic hypertension | HTN before 20 wk or persisting > 12 wk postpartum |
  | Gestational hypertension | New HTN after 20 wk, **no** proteinuria, no severe features |
  | Preeclampsia | New HTN after 20 wk **+** proteinuria **or**, in its absence, specified end-organ findings |
  | Preeclampsia with severe features | See below |
  | Eclampsia | New-onset **generalized seizure** in preeclampsia |
  | Chronic HTN with superimposed preeclampsia | |
- **Severe features to memorize:** systolic ≥ 160 or diastolic ≥ 110 mm Hg on two occasions; thrombocytopenia; impaired liver function or persistent severe RUQ/epigastric pain; renal insufficiency; pulmonary edema; new-onset headache unresponsive to medication; visual disturbances.
- **The teaching point that saves lives:** proteinuria is **not required** to diagnose preeclampsia when severe features are present. A student waiting for a protein result while the patient reports a crushing headache and epigastric pain has already lost the case.
- **Prophylaxis:** low-dose aspirin for those at elevated risk, per ACOG guidance.
> *Sources:* ACOG (2020b) [PB 222, interim update]; ACOG (2018e) [CO 743]; Lowdermilk et al. (2024).

### 2B. HELLP syndrome — *Lab & Diagnostic Tests; Analyze Cues*
- **H**emolysis (↑ LDH, ↓ haptoglobin, schistocytes, ↑ indirect bilirubin) + **E**levated **L**iver enzymes (↑ AST/ALT) + **L**ow **P**latelets.
- **Presentation trap:** may present with malaise, nausea, and RUQ pain and **normal or only mildly elevated blood pressure**. Students who anchor on "preeclampsia = high BP" will miss it.
- **Complications:** DIC, abruption, hepatic hematoma/rupture, acute kidney injury, pulmonary edema.
- **Management:** stabilization, magnesium sulfate, antihypertensives, corticosteroids for fetal maturation if preterm and delivery is anticipated, and **delivery as the definitive treatment**.

### 2C. Magnesium sulfate — *Pharmacology & Parenteral Therapies; Safety*
Treat this as a mini-module; it appears on every exam.
- **Purpose:** **seizure prophylaxis/treatment**, not blood pressure control. Say this three times.
- **Monitoring:** deep tendon reflexes, respiratory rate, urine output (magnesium is renally cleared), level of consciousness, continuous fetal monitoring, serum magnesium as ordered.
- **Order of toxicity signs:** **loss of DTRs → respiratory depression → ECG changes → cardiac arrest.** DTRs disappear first; that is why you check them.
- **Antidote:** **calcium gluconate** — know where it is stored on the unit before you need it.
- **Antihypertensives for acute severe hypertension:** labetalol, hydralazine, or oral immediate-release nifedipine per protocol.
- **QSEN/Safety:** high-alert medication, independent double-check, dedicated pump channel, standardized concentration.

### 2D. Hyperemesis gravidarum — *Nutrition; Fluid/Electrolytes; Psychosocial*
- **Distinguish from normal NVP:** persistent vomiting with **weight loss (often > 5% of prepregnancy weight), dehydration, ketonuria, and electrolyte disturbance**. Severity can be quantified with the **PUQE** score.
- **Nursing actions:** IV rehydration, electrolyte correction, **thiamine supplementation before dextrose-containing fluids** (Wernicke encephalopathy risk), antiemetics (pyridoxine ± doxylamine first-line; escalate per protocol), small frequent meals on re-feeding, oral care, quiet environment, daily weights, strict intake and output.
- **Psychosocial:** HG is frequently minimized by clinicians and family. Validate; screen for depression and for the sense of isolation that accompanies prolonged illness.
> *Source:* ACOG (2018c) [PB 189].

### 2E. Gestational diabetes — *Nutrition; Pharmacology; Lifespan*
- **Screening:** two-step approach (50 g GCT → 100 g 3-hour OGTT) at 24–28 weeks; earlier if risk factors. **Verify current thresholds** — ACOG's 2024 Clinical Practice Update on diabetes screening updates portions of Practice Bulletin 190.
- **Classification:** A1 (nutrition-controlled) vs A2 (medication-requiring).
- **Management:** medical nutrition therapy, self-monitoring of blood glucose, exercise, insulin as first-line pharmacotherapy when needed.
- **Maternal risks:** preeclampsia, polyhydramnios, cesarean birth, future type 2 diabetes.
- **Fetal/neonatal risks:** macrosomia and LGA, shoulder dystocia and birth trauma, **neonatal hypoglycemia**, hyperbilirubinemia, RDS, later-life metabolic risk.
- **Intrapartum:** glucose monitoring; **neonatal glucose check shortly after birth** — the newborn of a diabetic mother is hyperinsulinemic and drops fast once the maternal supply stops.
- **Postpartum:** insulin requirements fall abruptly after placental delivery; arrange postpartum glucose tolerance screening.
> *Sources:* ACOG (2018b) [PB 190]; ACOG (2024) [Clinical Practice Update]; Lowdermilk et al. (2024).

### Active learning
- **AL1 — Trend case:** four sets of serial vitals and labs across 36 hours; students identify the point at which the picture becomes preeclampsia with severe features and state the action.
- **AL2 — Magnesium safety drill:** simulated assessment findings; students decide continue / hold / call / give calcium gluconate.

**Exit ticket:** First sign of magnesium toxicity, and the antidote.

# DAY 3 (Wednesday, Week 2) — Preterm Labor, PROM/PPROM, Induction, Dysfunctional Labor

**Topics:** preterm labor · PROM and PPROM · antenatal corticosteroids · induction and augmentation · dystocia

### Objectives
1. Identify risk factors and clinical cues for preterm labor and describe tocolytic and corticosteroid therapy.
2. Differentiate PROM from PPROM and prioritize nursing care for each.
3. Describe indications, methods, and nursing responsibilities for induction and augmentation of labor.
4. Analyze dysfunctional labor using the 5 Ps and current labor-arrest criteria.

### 3A. Preterm labor and birth — *Risk Factors; Vulnerable Populations*
- **Scale:** approximately **1 in 10 U.S. newborns** is born preterm; these newborns account for roughly three-quarters of perinatal mortality and more than half of long-term neonatal morbidity.
- **Health equity — teach this, do not skip it:** ACOG's Practice Bulletin 234 explicitly addresses **disparities in preterm birth rates across racial and ethnic groups**. Frame this as a structural and access issue, not a biological one, and connect it to the Vulnerable Populations thread.
- **Risk factors:** prior spontaneous preterm birth (strongest), short cervical length, multiple gestation, uterine anomaly, infection, short interpregnancy interval, substance use, low socioeconomic status and inadequate prenatal care access.
- **Screening/prediction:** transvaginal cervical length; fetal fibronectin (most useful for its **negative** predictive value).
- **Prevention:** vaginal progesterone, cerclage in selected patients. **Note:** 17-OHPC (Makena) was withdrawn from the U.S. market in 2023 — confirm what the department's textbook says and correct it if it is outdated.
- **Tocolytics — purpose is to buy 48 hours for steroids and transfer, not to stop labor indefinitely:**
  | Agent | Class | Key nursing monitoring |
  |---|---|---|
  | Nifedipine | Calcium channel blocker | Hypotension, headache |
  | Indomethacin | NSAID | < 32 wk, limited duration; oligohydramnios, ductal constriction |
  | Terbutaline | Beta-agonist | Maternal tachycardia, hyperglycemia, pulmonary edema; **not for prolonged use** |
  | Magnesium sulfate | — | Used for **fetal neuroprotection** < 32 wk, not primarily as a tocolytic |
- **Antenatal corticosteroids (ACOG CO 713):** a single course is recommended for pregnant patients between **24 0/7 and 33 6/7 weeks** at risk of preterm delivery within 7 days, including those with ruptured membranes and multiple gestations; may be considered from **23 0/7 weeks** based on the family's resuscitation decision. Betamethasone **may be considered between 34 0/7 and 36 6/7 weeks** for those at risk of preterm birth within 7 days who have not had a prior course. A single repeat course may be considered before 34 0/7 weeks if the prior course was more than 14 days earlier.
> *Sources:* ACOG (2021) [PB 234]; ACOG (2016) [PB 171]; ACOG (2017a) [CO 713].

### 3B. PROM and PPROM — *Infection Control; Safety*
- **PROM** = rupture before labor onset **at or after 37 weeks**. **PPROM** = rupture before labor onset **before 37 weeks**.
- **Diagnosis:** sterile **speculum** examination with pooling, nitrazine, and ferning. **Minimize digital examinations** — each one increases infection risk and shortens the latency period.
- **Assessment priorities:** fluid color, odor, and amount; maternal temperature and pulse; fetal heart rate; uterine tenderness; confirm gestational age and fetal position. **Vaginal bleeding with ROM should raise concern for abruption.**
- **Management themes:** at term, induction shortens the rupture-to-birth interval and reduces chorioamnionitis and endometritis and NICU admission without increasing cesarean or operative vaginal birth. Preterm, expectant management with latency antibiotics and corticosteroids is generally pursued in the appropriate gestational-age window.
- **GBS:** prophylaxis is given based on prior culture results or on intrapartum risk factors when cultures are unavailable.
- **Complications to watch:** chorioamnionitis, **cord prolapse** (especially with an unengaged presenting part), abruption, oligohydramnios and cord compression, preterm birth.
> *Source:* ACOG (2020d) [PB 217].

### 3C. Induction and augmentation of labor — *Pharmacology; Safety; Informatics*
- **Indications:** postterm, PROM, hypertensive disease, FGR, diabetes, chorioamnionitis, IUFD, and elective induction at or after 39 weeks in appropriate candidates.
- **Contraindications:** placenta previa, vasa previa, transverse lie, prior classical uterine incision, active genital herpes, cord prolapse.
- **Bishop score:** predicts likelihood of successful induction; a low score signals the need for cervical ripening first.
- **Methods:** mechanical (transcervical balloon, membrane sweeping, amniotomy) and pharmacologic (misoprostol, dinoprostone, oxytocin).
- **Oxytocin nursing responsibilities:** high-alert medication; secondary line via pump; titrate to contraction pattern and fetal response, not to a target dose; continuous EFM; assess for **tachysystole**.
- **Tachysystole response:** reduce or stop oxytocin, reposition (left lateral), IV fluid bolus, consider terbutaline per protocol, notify the provider.
- **Failed induction criteria (ACOG/SMFM Obstetric Care Consensus No. 1):** the latent phase persisting despite cervical ripening and **at least 24 hours of oxytocin with ruptured membranes** — before that, "failed" is premature. This consensus statement was a national call to reduce the primary cesarean rate.
> *Sources:* ACOG & SMFM (2014) [OCC No. 1]; Lowdermilk et al. (2024).

### 3D. Dysfunctional labor (dystocia) — *Analyze Cues; Multidisciplinary*
- **Framework — the 5 Ps:** Passenger (size, presentation, position), Passageway (pelvis, soft tissue), Powers (contraction strength/coordination), Position (maternal), Psyche (fear, exhaustion, unrelieved pain — a legitimate cause of dysfunctional labor, not a soft factor).
- **Patterns:** hypotonic vs hypertonic dysfunction; protraction vs arrest; first- vs second-stage arrest.
- **Contemporary criteria shift:** ACOG/SMFM recognized that the historic Friedman curve overstates normal progress; **slow but progressive labor in the first stage is not an indication for cesarean**, and adequate time must be allowed in the second stage before diagnosing arrest.
- **Most common indications for primary cesarean, in order:** **labor dystocia**, abnormal or indeterminate FHR tracing, fetal malpresentation, multiple gestation, and suspected macrosomia.
- **Nursing interventions:** position changes and ambulation, hydration, pain relief, emptying the bladder, peanut ball, one-to-one support, IUPC for MVU assessment when indicated, oxytocin augmentation, amniotomy.
- **Multidisciplinary:** anesthesia, provider, surgical team, neonatal team, interpreter, doula, social work.
> *Source:* ACOG & SMFM (2014); Lowdermilk et al. (2024).

### Active learning
- **AL1 — PPROM case:** 30 weeks, gush of fluid. Students order their first five actions and justify why a digital exam is not among them.
- **AL2 — Oxytocin tachysystole simulation:** students execute the intrauterine resuscitation sequence and hand off via SBAR.

**Exit ticket:** Two nursing actions for tachysystole, and the gestational-age window for a single course of antenatal corticosteroids.

# DAY 4 (Thursday, Week 2) — Operative Birth, FGR, Uterine Rupture, Cord Emergencies, Compromised Neonate, Fetal Demise

**Topics:** operative vaginal and cesarean birth · fetal growth restriction · uterine rupture · cord compression and prolapse · the compromised neonate · fetal demise and bereavement

### Objectives
1. Describe indications, prerequisites, and complications of operative vaginal and cesarean birth.
2. Explain fetal growth restriction and its surveillance.
3. Recognize uterine rupture and initiate emergency response.
4. Interpret FHR categories and execute intrauterine resuscitation for cord compression and prolapse.
5. Anticipate and support resuscitation of the compromised neonate.
6. Provide culturally responsive, ethically sound bereavement care after fetal demise.

### 4A. Operative deliveries — *Surgery; Multidisciplinary; Teaching*
- **Operative vaginal birth (forceps, vacuum):** accounts for a small but important share of births. Prerequisites: fully dilated cervix, ruptured membranes, engaged head with known position, adequate anesthesia, empty bladder, informed consent, and a willingness to abandon the attempt.
- **Complications:** maternal laceration and hematoma; neonatal cephalohematoma, subgaleal hemorrhage (vacuum), facial nerve injury (forceps), hyperbilirubinemia.
- **Cesarean birth nursing care:** preoperative verification and consent, antibiotic prophylaxis timing, thromboprophylaxis, left uterine displacement on the table, postoperative hemorrhage and infection surveillance, pain management, early mobilization, breastfeeding support, and the emotional processing of an unplanned surgical birth.
- **TOLAC/VBAC:** requires a facility able to perform emergency cesarean; prior **classical** incision is a contraindication.
- **QI thread:** the **NTSV cesarean rate** is the standard national quality metric — connect it to the OCC No. 1 recommendations from Day 3.
> *Sources:* ACOG (2020f) [PB 219]; ACOG (2019) [PB 205]; ACOG & SMFM (2014).

### 4B. Fetal growth restriction (IUGR/FGR) — *Recognize Cues; Informatics*
- **Definition:** sonographic estimated fetal weight or abdominal circumference **below the 10th percentile** for gestational age. **FGR is the preferred current term**; IUGR persists in older sources and in this course's topic list.
- **Symmetric vs asymmetric:** symmetric suggests an early insult (aneuploidy, infection, teratogen); asymmetric ("head-sparing") suggests later uteroplacental insufficiency.
- **Etiologies:** maternal (hypertension, diabetes with vasculopathy, autoimmune disease, malnutrition, substance use), placental (insufficiency, abruption, previa), fetal (aneuploidy, congenital infection, multiple gestation).
- **Surveillance:** serial growth ultrasounds, amniotic fluid assessment, **umbilical artery Doppler** — absent or reversed end-diastolic flow signals severe compromise and escalates timing of delivery.
- **Neonatal consequences:** hypoglycemia, hypothermia, polycythemia, meconium aspiration, perinatal asphyxia.
- **Nursing:** fetal movement counting education, appointment adherence support (transportation, work leave — an economic thread), preparation for possible preterm birth.
> *Source:* ACOG (2021b) [PB 227].

### 4C. Uterine rupture — *Emergency/Critical Thinking*
- **Risk factors:** prior uterine surgery (especially classical incision), TOLAC, oxytocin hyperstimulation, grand multiparity, trauma, obstructed labor.
- **Cues — teach the cluster, since no single sign is reliable:** sudden **abnormal FHR (often prolonged deceleration or bradycardia — the most common and earliest sign)**, sudden severe abdominal pain, loss of station/receding presenting part, cessation of contractions, palpable fetal parts through the abdomen, vaginal bleeding, maternal tachycardia and hypotension.
- **Response:** call for help, activate emergency cesarean, large-bore IV access, oxygen, position, notify blood bank, prepare for possible hysterectomy, and support the family through an event that happens in minutes.

### 4D. Cord compression and prolapse — *the fastest emergency in the unit*
- **FHR interpretation, three-tier NICHD system:** **Category I** normal; **Category II** indeterminate (the large majority of tracings); **Category III** abnormal and predictive of abnormal fetal acid-base status.
- **VEAL CHOP:** **V**ariable decelerations → **C**ord compression; **E**arly → **H**ead compression; **A**ccelerations → **O**kay; **L**ate → **P**lacental insufficiency.
- **Intrauterine resuscitation for a Category II tracing** (per ACOG's current guideline): maternal position change, amnioinfusion, IV fluid bolus, reduction or cessation of oxytocin, and correction of maternal pathophysiology — **before** proceeding to cesarean. Current ACOG guidance **recommends against routine maternal oxygen administration** for Category II or III tracings in the absence of maternal hypoxia; this reverses long-standing teaching, so state it explicitly and expect pushback from clinical instructors.
- **Category III unresponsive to resuscitation → expedited delivery.**
- **Cord prolapse — memorize the sequence:**
  1. Call for help immediately.
  2. **Manually elevate the presenting part off the cord with a gloved hand and do not remove it** until delivery.
  3. Position: knee-chest or Trendelenburg / exaggerated Sims.
  4. Do not attempt to replace the cord; keep it moist if visible.
  5. Continuous FHR monitoring; prepare for immediate cesarean.
  6. Discontinue oxytocin; consider tocolysis per order.
- **Risk factors:** ROM with an unengaged presenting part, malpresentation, polyhydramnios, prematurity, long cord, multiple gestation.
> *Sources:* Macones et al. (2008); ACOG (2025) [Clinical Practice Guideline, intrapartum FHR monitoring]; Lowdermilk et al. (2024).

### 4E. The compromised neonate — *Multidisciplinary; Emergency*
- **Anticipate, do not react:** every complication in this unit is a reason to have the neonatal team at the birth.
- **NRP framework (AAP/AHA):** initial assessment — term? tone? breathing or crying? Then warm, dry, stimulate, position airway; **positive pressure ventilation is the single most important intervention** for a neonate who is apneic or bradycardic; escalate to chest compressions and epinephrine per algorithm.
- **Apgar scoring** describes the response to resuscitation; it does not direct it and does not diagnose asphyxia.
- **Conditions to recognize:** RDS, transient tachypnea, meconium aspiration, hypoglycemia (especially infants of diabetic mothers and FGR infants), hypothermia, polycythemia, sepsis, hypoxic-ischemic encephalopathy and candidacy for therapeutic hypothermia, neonatal opioid withdrawal.
- **Cord gases:** obtained after complicated births to document acid-base status.
- **Family-centered care:** narrate what is happening, keep the parents informed, facilitate contact as soon as it is safe.
> *Sources:* Weiner & Zaichkin (2021) [NRP, 8th ed. — **verify current edition**]; Lowdermilk et al. (2024).

### 4F. Fetal demise and bereavement — *Coping; Compassionate Caring; Culture; Ethics*
- **Scale:** stillbirth occurs in approximately **1 in 160 deliveries** in the United States.
- **Risk factors identified by ACOG:** nulliparity, advanced maternal age, obesity, preexisting diabetes, chronic hypertension, smoking, alcohol use, pregnancy achieved with assisted reproductive technology, multiple gestation, male fetal sex, past obstetric history, and **non-Hispanic Black race** — teach this last one as a marker of structural inequity and differential care access, not as biology. This is a Vulnerable Populations and health-equity teaching moment, and it should be named rather than read past.
- **Evaluation after stillbirth:** fetal autopsy; gross and histologic examination of the placenta, cord, and membranes; and genetic evaluation. Method and timing of delivery depend on gestational age, obstetric history, and maternal preference.
- **Nursing care — the highest-skill soft work in the unit:**
  - Follow the family's lead on seeing and holding the baby; offer, do not insist, and offer more than once.
  - Memory-making: photographs, hand and footprints, lock of hair, blanket, naming.
  - Language: use the baby's name; avoid "at least," avoid "you can have another."
  - Do not leave the family alone unless they ask; do not fill silence.
  - Lactation counseling — milk will come in, and no one warns them.
  - **Referral to a bereavement counselor, peer support group, or mental health professional** may be advisable for grief and depression.
  - **Cultural/spiritual:** ask about religious rites, burial or cremation practices, who should be present, and who should be told. Offer chaplaincy. Offer materials in Spanish.
  - **Ethical/Legal:** informed consent for autopsy, documentation, fetal death certificate requirements, and Texas disposition rules — verify current statute with the department.
  - **Staff:** debrief. Name that this care costs the nurse something.
> *Source:* ACOG (2020g) [Obstetric Care Consensus No. 10].

### Active learning
- **AL1 — Cord prolapse simulation:** three-minute timed drill, roles assigned, debriefed against the sequence above.
- **AL2 — Bereavement communication practice:** scripted phrases to use and to avoid; students practice aloud in pairs. This is uncomfortable and that is the point.
- **Wrap-up:** unit synthesis using the Hemorrhage / Hypertension / Hypoxia map; exam blueprint walkthrough.

**Exit ticket:** First two nursing actions for cord prolapse; one phrase you will never say to a bereaved parent.

## Reference-quality notes for faculty

1. **Three citations require verification before you teach — flagged deliberately.**
   - **ACOG intrapartum FHR guideline (2025).** The journal record and the guideline PDF disagree on the document number (one shows "No. 10," the other "No. 22"). The title, journal, volume, page range, and DOI are consistent. Confirm the correct number on acog.org before printing student handouts. This document supersedes Practice Bulletins 106 (2009) and 116 (2010).
   - **NRP edition.** The 8th edition is cited. Verify whether a 9th edition is now current, and match whatever the clinical sites use.
   - **17-OHPC (Makena).** Withdrawn from the U.S. market in 2023. Many textbook editions still list it as standard preterm-birth prophylaxis. Check the department's textbook and correct it in class if needed.

2. **Two documents supersede older material students may have been taught.**
   - ACOG's **2024 Clinical Practice Update** on diabetes screening updates portions of Practice Bulletin 190.
   - The **2025 intrapartum FHR guideline** recommends **against routine maternal oxygen** for Category II or III tracings absent maternal hypoxia. This contradicts what many clinical preceptors still practice. Teach the current recommendation and name the discrepancy so students are not blindsided on the unit.

3. **Corporate authorship.** ACOG documents are organization-authored. Some departments prefer ACOG's in-house style (e.g., "Gestational hypertension and preeclampsia. ACOG Practice Bulletin No. 222. *Obstet Gynecol* 2020;135:e237–60."). Confirm the department's preference for student handouts.

4. **Textbook editions are placeholders.** Replace with the department's adopted editions — page alignment with the exam matters more than recency.

5. **Deliberately excluded:** UpToDate, commercial NCLEX prep publishers, Wikipedia, Studocu and similar note-sharing sites, nursing blogs, video channels, and secondary sites restating ACOG content.

6. **Race in this unit.** Two cited guidelines (PB 234 on preterm birth, OCC No. 10 on stillbirth) list Black race among risk factors. Teach these as markers of structural racism, differential access, and quality-of-care disparities — not as biological causation. Saying nothing is worse than saying it carefully.
