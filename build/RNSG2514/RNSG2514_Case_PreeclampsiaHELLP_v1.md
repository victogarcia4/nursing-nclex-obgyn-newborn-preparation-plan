# Unfolding Case (WO-G) — Preeclampsia Progressing to HELLP
**Course:** RNSG 2514 Nursing III · **Traces to objectives:** 2514-D2-2, 2514-D2-3, 2514-D2-4 · **Pathway:** Hypertension · **Format:** 6 scenes, one per NCJMM operation.

**Patient:** Grace, 34, G1 P0, at 35 weeks, admitted with elevated blood pressure.

---

### Scene 1 — Recognize Cues (highlight)
**Chart (admission):** BP 158/104, reports a headache and right upper quadrant discomfort, platelets 130,000, AST 40, urine protein-to-creatinine ratio 0.5.
**Item.** Highlight the cues indicating preeclampsia.
**Key:** BP ≥ 140/90 after 20 weeks + proteinuria (P:C 0.5) + headache and RUQ discomfort.
**Rationale.** Hypertension after 20 weeks with proteinuria and symptoms establishes preeclampsia [ACOG PB 222].

### Scene 2 — Analyze Cues (matrix)
**New labs (6 hours later):** platelets 88,000; AST 160; LDH 700; haptoglobin low; BP 166/112.
**Item.** For each new value, mark *Indicates a severe feature / HELLP* or *Not concerning*.

| Finding | Answer |
|---|---|
| Platelets 88,000 | Severe feature / HELLP |
| AST 160 | Severe feature / HELLP |
| LDH 700, low haptoglobin | Hemolysis (HELLP) |
| BP 166/112 | Severe-range BP |

**Rationale.** Falling platelets, rising transaminases, and hemolysis markers signal progression to HELLP [ACOG PB 222].

### Scene 3 — Prioritize Hypotheses (cloze/dropdown)
**Item.** The evolving diagnosis is **[preeclampsia with severe features / HELLP]**, and the priority interventions are **[seizure prophylaxis and blood pressure control with delivery planning]**.
**Rationale.** HELLP is a severe-features variant requiring magnesium, antihypertensives, and timing of birth [ACOG PB 222].

### Scene 4 — Generate Solutions (extended multiple response)
**Item.** Select the 3 correct interventions.
- ✅ Begin magnesium sulfate for seizure prophylaxis
- ✅ Give an antihypertensive (labetalol, hydralazine, or nifedipine) for severe-range BP
- ✅ Prepare for delivery planning and give corticosteroids if indicated
- ❌ Use magnesium sulfate to lower the blood pressure
- ❌ Discharge home with oral iron
- ❌ Withhold treatment until proteinuria is quantified by 24-hour urine

**Rationale.** Magnesium prevents seizures; separate antihypertensives lower BP; do not delay for a 24-hour urine when severe features are present [ACOG PB 222].

### Scene 5 — Take Actions (SATA)
**Item.** While starting magnesium, which nursing actions apply? Select all that apply.
- ✅ Keep calcium gluconate at the bedside
- ✅ Monitor deep tendon reflexes, respirations, and urine output
- ✅ Assess for pulmonary edema and continuous fetal monitoring
- ✅ Reassess BP and treat severe-range values promptly
- ❌ Expect magnesium to normalize the blood pressure

**Rationale.** Magnesium safety monitoring plus separate BP control are both required [ACOG PB 222].

### Scene 6 — Evaluate Outcomes (trend)
**Post-delivery trend:** hour 0 platelets 84,000, AST 160; day 1 platelets 110,000, AST 90; day 2 platelets 150,000, AST 45; BP improving on therapy; reflexes 2+, respirations 16.
**Item.** Which interpretation best evaluates recovery?
**Key:** Platelets are rising and transaminases falling after delivery — HELLP is resolving and magnesium monitoring shows no toxicity.
**Rationale.** Evaluate HELLP recovery by the platelet/enzyme trend post-delivery, the definitive treatment [ACOG PB 222].

---

## Debrief — three decision points students most often miss
1. **Expecting magnesium to lower BP.** Magnesium is seizure prophylaxis; severe-range BP needs a separate antihypertensive.
2. **Waiting for proteinuria quantification.** Severe features (thrombocytopenia, RUQ pain, transaminases) justify acting now.
3. **Missing the HELLP trend.** A single platelet value can mislead; the falling platelet/rising enzyme trajectory is the alarm.
