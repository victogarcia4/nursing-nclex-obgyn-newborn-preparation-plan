# Unfolding Case (WO-G) — GDM in Labor and Neonatal Hypoglycemia
**Course:** RNSG 2514 Nursing III · **Traces to objectives:** 2514-D2-7, 2514-D4-6 · **Threads:** Lifespan (neonate as a distinct patient) · **Format:** 6 scenes, one per NCJMM operation.

**Patient:** Ana, 33, G2 P1, at 39 weeks with medication-requiring gestational diabetes (class A2), now in labor. Fetus estimated large for gestational age.

---

### Scene 1 — Recognize Cues (highlight)
**Chart (intrapartum):** Class A2 GDM on insulin; intrapartum glucose 150 mg/dL; estimated fetal weight > 90th percentile; labor progressing.
**Item.** Highlight the cues that predict neonatal hypoglycemia risk.
**Key:** maternal hyperglycemia in labor + LGA fetus (maternal glucose drives fetal hyperinsulinism).
**Rationale.** Sustained maternal hyperglycemia causes fetal hyperinsulinism, setting up newborn hypoglycemia after cord clamping [ACOG PB 190].

### Scene 2 — Analyze Cues (matrix)
**Item.** For each newborn finding, mark *Consistent with hypoglycemia* or *Reassuring*.

| Newborn finding (first hour) | Answer |
|---|---|
| Jitteriness/tremors | Consistent with hypoglycemia |
| Poor feeding, lethargy | Consistent with hypoglycemia |
| Point-of-care glucose 32 mg/dL | Consistent with hypoglycemia |
| Vigorous cry, glucose 55 mg/dL | Reassuring |

**Rationale.** Jitteriness, poor feeding, and low glucose signal neonatal hypoglycemia in an infant of a diabetic mother [ACOG PB 190].

### Scene 3 — Prioritize Hypotheses (cloze/dropdown)
**Item.** After birth, the priority for this LGA infant of a mother with A2 GDM is **[early glucose screening]** because the risk is **[neonatal hypoglycemia from fetal hyperinsulinism]**.
**Rationale.** Anticipate and screen; do not wait for florid symptoms [ACOG PB 190].

### Scene 4 — Generate Solutions (extended multiple response)
**Item.** Select the 3 correct newborn management steps.
- ✅ Initiate early feeding (breast or formula) per protocol
- ✅ Check point-of-care blood glucose on schedule
- ✅ Treat confirmed hypoglycemia per protocol and recheck
- ❌ Withhold feeding for several hours
- ❌ Ignore a glucose of 32 mg/dL in an asymptomatic infant
- ❌ Give the mother's insulin dose to the newborn

**Rationale.** Early feeding and scheduled glucose checks prevent and treat hypoglycemia [ACOG PB 190].

### Scene 5 — Take Actions (drag-and-drop ordering)
**Scenario:** First glucose is 32 mg/dL with jitteriness.
**Item.** Order the nursing actions.
1. Recognize the low glucose and symptoms
2. Feed the infant per protocol (or give ordered dextrose if unable to feed)
3. Recheck the glucose after the intervention
4. Escalate to the provider if the glucose remains low
5. Document values, interventions, and response

**Rationale.** Feed-then-recheck with escalation is the standard hypoglycemia response [ACOG PB 190].

### Scene 6 — Evaluate Outcomes (trend)
**Neonatal glucose trend:** hour 1 — 32 mg/dL (fed); hour 2 — 46 mg/dL (rechecked, refed); hour 4 — 58 mg/dL, infant feeding well and no tremors.
**Item.** Which interpretation best evaluates the response?
**Key:** Glucose is rising into the acceptable range with feeding — the hypoglycemia is resolving and the plan is working.
**Rationale.** Evaluate neonatal hypoglycemia management by the glucose trend and resolving symptoms across serial checks [ACOG PB 190].

---

## Debrief — three decision points students most often miss
1. **Connecting maternal glucose to the newborn.** The mother's intrapartum control directly predicts newborn hypoglycemia.
2. **Waiting for symptoms.** Screen the at-risk infant early; a low value in an "asymptomatic" infant still needs action.
3. **Delaying feeding.** Early feeding is the first-line prevention and treatment; do not withhold it.
