# Unfolding Case (WO-G) — Uterine Rupture During TOLAC
**Course:** RNSG 2514 Nursing III · **Traces to objectives:** 2514-D4-3, 2514-D3-4 · **Pathway:** Hemorrhage/Hypoxia · **Format:** 6 scenes, one per NCJMM operation.

**Patient:** Camila, 32, G2 P1, at 40 weeks, attempting a trial of labor after one prior low-transverse cesarean (TOLAC). Oxytocin augmentation running.

---

### Scene 1 — Recognize Cues (highlight)
**Chart:** Sudden severe, tearing abdominal pain reported between contractions; the fetal station, previously 0, is now higher; FHR drops to the 60s–70s (prolonged bradycardia); maternal HR rising, BP falling.
**Item.** Highlight the cues of uterine rupture.
**Key:** acute tearing pain + loss of station (rising presenting part) + prolonged fetal bradycardia during TOLAC.
**Rationale.** This triad during TOLAC is uterine rupture until proven otherwise [ACOG PB 205].

### Scene 2 — Analyze Cues (matrix)
**Item.** For each finding, mark *Consistent with rupture* or *Expected labor finding*.

| Finding | Answer |
|---|---|
| Sudden tearing abdominal pain | Consistent with rupture |
| Loss of fetal station | Consistent with rupture |
| Prolonged fetal bradycardia | Consistent with rupture |
| Rhythmic contractions with descent | Expected labor finding |

**Rationale.** Rupture disrupts the normal labor pattern with pain, lost station, and fetal compromise [ACOG PB 205].

### Scene 3 — Prioritize Hypotheses (cloze/dropdown)
**Item.** The emergency is **[uterine rupture]**, and the priority is **[immediate preparation for emergency cesarean]** with readiness for **[neonatal resuscitation and maternal hemorrhage]**.
**Rationale.** Rupture is a dual emergency — fetal hypoxia and maternal bleeding — demanding immediate surgery [ACOG PB 205].

### Scene 4 — Generate Solutions (extended multiple response)
**Item.** Select the 3 correct immediate actions.
- ✅ Stop the oxytocin infusion
- ✅ Call for help and prepare for emergency cesarean
- ✅ Begin maternal resuscitation measures (IV fluids, prepare for transfusion)
- ❌ Increase oxytocin to deliver faster
- ❌ Continue expectant management
- ❌ Delay surgery to obtain a formal ultrasound first

**Rationale.** Stop stimulation, mobilize surgery, and resuscitate; delay costs the fetus and mother [ACOG PB 205].

### Scene 5 — Take Actions (SATA)
**Item.** Which nursing actions apply now? Select all that apply.
- ✅ Discontinue oxytocin immediately
- ✅ Notify the surgical and neonatal teams
- ✅ Establish large-bore IV access and prepare blood products
- ✅ Position for optimal perfusion and give oxygen if maternal hypoxia is present
- ❌ Administer a uterotonic to augment contractions

**Rationale.** Every action supports the dual emergency; augmenting contractions is contraindicated [ACOG PB 205].

### Scene 6 — Evaluate Outcomes (trend)
**Trend:** minute 0 FHR 65, maternal HR 118, BP 92/54; minute 10 in OR, delivery accomplished; post-op maternal HR 96, BP 110/68 after fluids/blood; neonate resuscitated, Apgar 4→8.
**Item.** Which interpretation best evaluates the response?
**Key:** Rapid recognition and emergency cesarean with resuscitation stabilized mother and infant — the escalation was timely and effective.
**Rationale.** Evaluate by time-to-delivery, maternal hemodynamic recovery, and neonatal response [ACOG PB 205].

---

## Debrief — three decision points students most often miss
1. **Attributing the pain to labor.** Tearing pain *between* contractions with lost station is not normal labor.
2. **Continuing or increasing oxytocin.** Stimulation worsens a rupturing uterus; stop it immediately.
3. **Delaying for imaging.** Suspected rupture goes to the OR; do not wait for a formal ultrasound.
