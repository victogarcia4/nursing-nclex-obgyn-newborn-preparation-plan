# Unfolding Case (WO-G) — Umbilical Cord Prolapse
**Course:** RNSG 2514 Nursing III · **Traces to objectives:** 2514-D4-4, 2514-D4-5 · **Pathway:** Hypoxia · **Format:** 6 scenes, one per NCJMM operation.

**Patient:** Nia, 25, G1 P0, at 39 weeks in active labor. Fetus at a high station; artificial rupture of membranes just performed.

---

### Scene 1 — Recognize Cues (highlight)
**Chart:** Immediately after rupture, the FHR drops to 70 and stays low; the nurse palpates a pulsating cord in the vagina; the presenting part is high.
**Item.** Highlight the cues of cord prolapse.
**Key:** abrupt fetal bradycardia after ROM + a palpable pulsating cord + high presenting part.
**Rationale.** Bradycardia right after rupture with a palpable cord is prolapse — the fastest emergency in the unit [Lowdermilk].

### Scene 2 — Analyze Cues (matrix)
**Item.** For each factor, mark *Raises prolapse risk* or *Not a risk factor here*.

| Factor | Answer |
|---|---|
| High/unengaged presenting part at ROM | Raises prolapse risk |
| Rupture of membranes | Raises prolapse risk |
| Fetal bradycardia after ROM | Consequence to act on |
| Reactive FHR before ROM | Not a risk factor |

**Rationale.** An unengaged part at ROM is the classic setup; prolapse compresses the cord and causes bradycardia [Lowdermilk].

### Scene 3 — Prioritize Hypotheses (cloze/dropdown)
**Item.** The emergency is **[umbilical cord prolapse]**, and the priority is to **[relieve cord compression and expedite birth]**.
**Rationale.** Every second of compression risks fetal hypoxia; relieving it comes first [Lowdermilk].

### Scene 4 — Generate Solutions (extended multiple response)
**Item.** Select the 3 correct immediate solutions.
- ✅ Manually elevate the presenting part off the cord with a gloved hand
- ✅ Reposition to knee-chest or steep Trendelenburg
- ✅ Call for help and prepare for immediate cesarean birth
- ❌ Attempt to push the cord back into the uterus
- ❌ Increase oxytocin to speed delivery
- ❌ Place the patient supine and flat

**Rationale.** Offload the cord and reposition while mobilizing surgery; never handle or replace the cord [Lowdermilk].

### Scene 5 — Take Actions (drag-and-drop ordering)
**Item.** Order the emergency actions.
1. Call for help and press the emergency call light
2. Use a gloved hand to lift the presenting part off the cord
3. Place the patient in knee-chest or steep Trendelenburg
4. Prepare for immediate cesarean birth
5. Keep the examining hand in place during transfer to the OR

**Rationale.** The hand that finds the prolapse stays to protect perfusion until delivery [Lowdermilk].

### Scene 6 — Evaluate Outcomes (trend)
**Trend:** minute 0 FHR 70 with cord compression; minute 2 after manual elevation and repositioning, FHR 120; minute 8 in the OR, FHR 140; infant delivered by cesarean with Apgar 8/9.
**Item.** Which interpretation best evaluates the response?
**Key:** Manual elevation restored fetal perfusion (FHR recovered), and expedited cesarean delivered a vigorous infant — the emergency response was effective.
**Rationale.** Evaluate the intervention by FHR recovery once compression is relieved and by neonatal condition at birth [Lowdermilk].

---

## Debrief — three decision points students most often miss
1. **Removing the hand.** The examining hand must stay to keep the presenting part off the cord until birth.
2. **Handling the cord.** Pushing it back or manipulating it causes vasospasm and worsens hypoxia.
3. **Wrong position.** Supine-flat worsens compression; knee-chest or Trendelenburg offloads the cord.
