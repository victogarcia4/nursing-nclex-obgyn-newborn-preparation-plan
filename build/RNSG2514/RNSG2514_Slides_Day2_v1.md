## Slide 5 - Classification depends on timing and end-organ findings
| Disorder | Defining frame |
|---|---|
| chronic hypertension | before 20 weeks or persists >12 weeks postpartum |
| gestational hypertension | new after 20 weeks without proteinuria/severe features |
| preeclampsia | new hypertension plus proteinuria or end-organ findings |
| eclampsia | new generalized seizure in preeclampsia |

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask why timing matters. Common error: using the term PIH instead of current classification. [ACOG PB 222]

## Slide 6 - Proteinuria is not required when severe features are present
- Severe-range BP is a key cue.
- Other cues: thrombocytopenia, liver dysfunction/RUQ pain, renal insufficiency, pulmonary edema, persistent headache, visual disturbance.
- Stabilize and escalate the cue cluster.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask, "What action is unsafe if you wait for protein?" Common error: treating proteinuria as the gatekeeper to urgent care. [ACOG PB 222]

## Slide 7 - HELLP is a lab pattern that may hide behind nonspecific symptoms
- Hemolysis: increased LDH, low haptoglobin, schistocytes, indirect bilirubin rise.
- Elevated liver enzymes.
- Low platelets.
- Nausea, malaise, and RUQ pain can occur with only modest BP elevation.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask students to expand HELLP. Common error: dismissing RUQ pain because the BP is not markedly elevated. [ACOG PB 222]

## Slide 8 - Delivery is definitive treatment after stabilization in HELLP
- Stabilize maternal status and anticipate delivery planning.
- Magnesium sulfate, antihypertensives, and corticosteroids may be indicated in the clinical plan.
- Prepare for DIC, abruption, pulmonary edema, renal injury, and hepatic complications.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask which complication requires immediate surveillance. Common error: treating HELLP as a laboratory abnormality without obstetric urgency. [ACOG PB 222]

## Slide 9 - Magnesium sulfate prevents or treats seizures; it does not lower BP
- Indication: seizure prophylaxis or treatment.
- Monitor DTRs, respiratory rate, urine output, level of consciousness, and fetal status.
- Use high-alert medication safeguards and a dedicated pump channel.

> NOTES: Talk track: Have students repeat the purpose aloud. Common error: describing magnesium as the antihypertensive. [ACOG PB 222]

## Slide 10 - Magnesium toxicity follows a recognizable sequence
| Finding sequence | Nursing response |
|---|---|
| loss of DTRs | hold/escalate per protocol |
| respiratory depression | urgent escalation |
| ECG changes | emergency response |
| cardiac arrest | resuscitation response |
| antidote | calcium gluconate |

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask which assessment detects toxicity earliest. Common error: waiting for respiratory arrest before holding the infusion. [ACOG PB 222]

## Slide 11 - Acute severe hypertension has a protocol-driven response
- Labetalol, hydralazine, or oral immediate-release nifedipine may be used per protocol.
- Magnesium sulfate and antihypertensive treatment serve different purposes.
- Document timing, response, and escalation.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask students to state the two distinct treatment targets. Common error: assuming one medication solves both seizure and BP risk. [ACOG PB 222] [VERIFY: local order set]

## Slide 14 - Hyperemesis is more than common nausea and vomiting
- Persistent vomiting with weight loss, dehydration, ketonuria, or electrolyte disturbance indicates hyperemesis concern.
- The PUQE score can support severity assessment.
- Validate the psychosocial burden.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask which finding distinguishes hyperemesis from expected nausea. Common error: minimizing prolonged illness. [ACOG PB 189]

## Slide 15 - Thiamine must come before dextrose-containing fluids when indicated
- Rehydrate and correct electrolytes.
- Give thiamine before dextrose-containing fluids to reduce Wernicke encephalopathy risk.
- Track weight, intake/output, oral tolerance, and symptoms.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask students why sequence matters. Common error: starting dextrose without considering thiamine status. [ACOG PB 189]

## Slide 16 - GDM screening guidance has a required currency flag

- ACOG 2024 Clinical Practice Update supersedes parts of PB 190.

|---|---|
| A1 | nutrition-controlled |

## Slide 18 - Glycemic management protects both patients
- Medical nutrition therapy and self-monitoring are foundational.
- Exercise and insulin may be part of the plan.
- Maternal risks include preeclampsia, polyhydramnios, and cesarean birth.
- Management must fit food access, literacy, and schedule.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask, "What barrier might make a correct plan unusable?" Common error: calling a patient noncompliant without assessing access. [ACOG PB 190] [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 19 - The newborn of a diabetic mother needs anticipation, not surprise
- Fetal/neonatal risks include macrosomia, birth trauma, hypoglycemia, hyperbilirubinemia, and respiratory distress.
- Check neonatal glucose shortly after birth per nursery protocol.
- Intrapartum and postpartum plans require team handoff.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask why neonatal glucose falls after placental delivery. Common error: focusing on maternal glucose only. [ACOG PB 190] [Lowdermilk] [VERIFY: nursery protocol]

## Slide 20 - First action: possible magnesium toxicity
Scenario: A patient on magnesium sulfate has absent DTRs, RR 10/min, and urine output falling.

A. Continue because magnesium prevents seizures.
B. Hold the infusion, escalate urgently, and prepare calcium gluconate per protocol.
C. Give an antihypertensive first.
D. Reassess at the next scheduled hour.

**Answer: B.** The toxicity cue cluster requires immediate action.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask which cue appeared first. Common error: treating seizure prevention as a reason to ignore toxicity. [ACOG PB 222]

## Slide 21 - The other options ignore the purpose or urgency of treatment
- A fails: toxicity can progress to respiratory and cardiac compromise.
- C fails: antihypertensive treatment does not reverse magnesium toxicity.
- D fails: waiting allows preventable deterioration.
- Transferable principle: high-alert medication monitoring must trigger action.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask teams to name the antidote and pump safeguard. Common error: mixing the indication, adverse effect, and antidote. [ACOG PB 222]

## Slide 22 - Active learning 2: magnesium safety drill
- Decide continue, hold, call, or give calcium gluconate from simulated findings.
- State the cue, action, and parameter to recheck.

> NOTES: Talk track: Repeat the drill until the sequence is fluent. Common error: naming the antidote but failing to hold/escalate. [ACOG PB 222]

## Slide 23 - Hypertension, hyperemesis, and GDM share a trend mindset
- Symptoms gain meaning with vital signs, labs, weights, glucose, and urine output.
- The nursing action changes when the trajectory changes.
- Reassessment is a clinical judgment step, not a closing task.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask students to give one example from each topic. Common error: managing one value without examining the trend. [ACOG PB 222] [ACOG PB 189] [ACOG PB 190]

## Slide 28 - A single cue rarely carries the full diagnosis
- BP alone does not classify every hypertensive disorder.
- Nausea alone does not prove hyperemesis.
- A glucose value must live in its screening and treatment context.
- Trends create the safer hypothesis.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask students to name the companion cue needed for each. Common error: anchoring on the most familiar datum. [ACOG PB 222] [ACOG PB 189] [ACOG PB 190]

## Slide 29 - Medication safety is a workflow, not a memory trick
- Confirm order, concentration, pump channel, and independent check.
- Assess before, during, and after administration.
- Know the antidote location and escalation pathway.
- Document the response and hand off the trend.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask which failure point is most likely during a busy shift. Common error: thinking a double-check replaces ongoing assessment. [ACOG PB 222]

## Slide 30 - Care plans must include the newborn and family
- GDM and hypertensive disease alter neonatal anticipation.
- Hyperemesis affects mental health and family functioning.
- Teach the patient what to report and how to access help.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask students to state one family-centered teaching point for each condition. Common error: ending the plan at maternal stabilization. [ACOG PB 190] [ACOG PB 189] [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 31 - NCJMM transfer: trend -> cluster -> prioritize -> act
- Recognize serial BP, symptoms, labs, output, glucose.
- Analyze the cluster rather than a single datum.
- Prioritize time-critical toxicity or severe-feature cues.
- Take action and evaluate the response.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask students to map the magnesium scenario. Common error: missing evaluation after the first action. [Dickison et al. 2019] [ACOG PB 222]

## Slide 32 - A reliable exit-ticket rationale names the cue and the action
- "Absent DTRs are the earliest toxicity cue; hold and escalate."
- "Hyperemesis includes dehydration/ketonuria or weight loss, not nausea alone."
- "A positive diabetes screen changes the next test, not the diagnosis."

## Slide 33 - Final synthesis: protect oxygen delivery and maternal safety
- Hypertension threatens cerebral, hepatic, renal, and placental function.
- Magnesium protects against seizures but requires vigilant monitoring.
- Nutrition and glucose plans prevent downstream maternal and neonatal harm.
