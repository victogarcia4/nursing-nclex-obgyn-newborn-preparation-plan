# Unfolding Case (WO-G) — PPROM at 30 Weeks
**Course:** RNSG 2514 Nursing III · **Traces to objectives:** 2514-D3-3, 2514-D3-1, 2514-D3-2 · **Pathway:** Hypoxia/Infection · **Format:** 6 scenes, one per NCJMM operation.

**Patient:** Leah, 27, G2 P1, at 30 weeks, reports a sudden gush of clear fluid.

---

### Scene 1 — Recognize Cues (highlight)
**Chart:** Gush of clear fluid; nitrazine turns blue; ferning seen on microscopy; temperature 37.0 °C; no uterine tenderness; no contractions; FHR 145 with moderate variability.
**Item.** Highlight the cues confirming prelabor rupture of membranes before term.
**Key:** blue nitrazine + ferning + gush of fluid at 30 weeks (preterm) = PPROM.
**Rationale.** Positive nitrazine and ferning confirm ROM; before 37 weeks it is PPROM [ACOG PB 217].

### Scene 2 — Analyze Cues (matrix)
**Item.** For each finding, mark *Reassuring* or *Sign of infection to watch*.

| Finding | Answer |
|---|---|
| Temperature 37.0 °C | Reassuring |
| No uterine tenderness | Reassuring |
| Maternal or fetal tachycardia (if it develops) | Sign of infection to watch |
| Foul-smelling discharge (if it develops) | Sign of infection to watch |

**Rationale.** Chorioamnionitis presents with fever, tenderness, and maternal/fetal tachycardia; none are present yet [ACOG PB 217].

### Scene 3 — Prioritize Hypotheses (cloze/dropdown)
**Item.** At 30 weeks without infection or labor, the management priority is **[expectant management to gain fetal maturity]**, while continuously watching for **[intraamniotic infection]**.
**Rationale.** Before 34 weeks, prematurity risk usually outweighs waiting — unless infection intervenes [ACOG PB 217].

### Scene 4 — Generate Solutions (extended multiple response)
**Item.** Select the 3 correct management elements.
- ✅ Administer antenatal corticosteroids for fetal lung maturity
- ✅ Give latency antibiotics per protocol
- ✅ Provide magnesium sulfate for neuroprotection if birth is imminent (< 32 weeks)
- ❌ Perform frequent digital cervical exams
- ❌ Induce labor immediately
- ❌ Discharge home with no monitoring

**Rationale.** Steroids, latency antibiotics, and neuroprotection are standard; digital exams are minimized to reduce infection [ACOG PB 217; CO 713].

### Scene 5 — Take Actions (SATA)
**Item.** Which nursing actions reduce infection risk and support the fetus? Select all that apply.
- ✅ Minimize digital vaginal examinations
- ✅ Monitor temperature, pulse, and FHR for infection signs
- ✅ Administer steroids and antibiotics as ordered
- ✅ Educate on reporting fever, foul discharge, or contractions
- ❌ Encourage tub bathing

**Rationale.** Fewer digital exams after ROM means fewer intraamniotic infections [ACOG PB 217].

### Scene 6 — Evaluate Outcomes (trend)
**Trend:** Day 1 afebrile, no contractions, reassuring FHR; Day 3 afebrile, steroid course complete; Day 5 develops temperature 38.4 °C, maternal HR 108, fetal baseline 170, uterine tenderness.
**Item.** Which interpretation should drive the next decision?
**Key:** Infection (chorioamnionitis) is now developing; expectant management ends and delivery with antibiotics is indicated.
**Rationale.** The trend shift to fever, tachycardia, and tenderness overrides latency — the fetus is safer delivered [ACOG PB 217].

---

## Debrief — three decision points students most often miss
1. **Doing digital exams "to check progress."** After ROM, each exam adds infection risk; minimize them.
2. **Treating latency as the fixed goal.** Latency ends the moment infection appears — reassess continuously.
3. **Forgetting neuroprotection.** Magnesium sulfate before 32 weeks reduces cerebral palsy and is easy to overlook.
