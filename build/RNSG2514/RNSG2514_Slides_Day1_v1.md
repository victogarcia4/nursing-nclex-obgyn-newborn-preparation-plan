## Bleeding Disorders, Pregnancy Loss, and Hemorrhage Response

> WO-C | 140 minutes | Markdown slide deck | Version v1

## Slide 1 - Bleeding becomes an emergency when the pathway is missed
- Day 1: early/late bleeding, pregnancy loss, hemorrhage, and DIC.
- Organizing pathway: hemorrhage.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask, "What action differs when the bleeding is painless rather than painful?" Common error: equating visible blood loss with total blood loss. [Lowdermilk] [ACOG PB 183]

## Slide 2 - These objectives define today's work
1. Differentiate causes of bleeding by presentation.
2. Distinguish placenta previa from abruption and state the action that differs.
3. Describe pregnancy-loss types and management options.
4. Prioritize hemorrhage actions, massive transfusion, and DIC recognition.
5. Apply ethical principles to loss documentation and abortion care.

> NOTES: Talk track: Read objectives verbatim. Common error: treating all pregnancy bleeding as the same triage problem. [ACOG PB 200] [ACOG PB 183] [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 4 - The warm-up asks for the first action, not the final diagnosis
- 32 weeks: painless bright-red bleeding; uterus soft.
- 32 weeks: painful dark bleeding; uterus rigid and tender.
- Which patient must not receive a digital vaginal examination?

> NOTES: Talk track: Give 3 minutes then ask for the cue that drove each answer. Common error: conducting a digital exam before previa is excluded. [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 5 - Pregnancy can mask early blood loss
- The term uterus receives substantial cardiac output.
- Physiologic hypervolemia can delay abnormal vital signs.
- Assess quantified blood loss, appearance, symptoms, and trends together.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask why normal early vital signs do not reassure in a hemorrhage pattern. Common error: waiting for hypotension before escalating. [Lowdermilk] [ACOG PB 183]

## Slide 6 - First-trimester bleeding requires cue-based differentiation
| Pattern | Key cue | Priority |
|---|---|---|
| threatened loss | closed cervix; viable IUP | assess, trend, support |
| incomplete/inevitable loss | open cervix; cramping/tissue | bleeding and management |
| ectopic pregnancy | unilateral pain, dizziness, shoulder pain | emergency assessment |
| gestational trophoblastic disease | very high hCG, hyperemesis, uterus large | surveillance/referral |

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask, "Which cue makes the ectopic pattern immediately dangerous?" Common error: assuming all early bleeding is stable. [ACOG PB 200] [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 7 - Early pregnancy loss has more than one patient-centered option
- Expectant, medical, and surgical management may be appropriate.
- Patient preference guides care when urgent evacuation is not required.
- Assess bleeding, infection signs, Rh status, pain, and emotional needs.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask students to name the information needed for informed choice. Common error: presenting one management path as the only ethical option. [ACOG PB 200]

## Slide 8 - Ectopic cues override routine pregnancy sequencing
- Unilateral pelvic pain, amenorrhea, bleeding, shoulder pain, dizziness, or hypotension are danger cues.
- Stabilize and escalate for possible hemorrhagic shock.
- Do not let a positive hCG replace a stability assessment.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask for the first two bedside assessments. Common error: scheduling routine follow-up for a potentially unstable patient. [ACOG PB 200] [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 9 - Placenta previa and abruption look different because the mechanism differs
| Feature | Placenta previa | Abruption |
|---|---|---|
| Bleeding | painless, bright red | painful, dark or concealed |
| Uterus | soft, relaxed | rigid, tender, hypertonic |
| Fetal pattern | may be initially reassuring | Category II/III may occur |
| Coagulopathy | uncommon | DIC risk |

> NOTES: Talk track: Have students identify the discriminating rows. Common error: relying on bleeding color alone. [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 10 - The action that differs is the exam you do not perform
- Suspected placenta previa: defer digital vaginal examination until excluded.
- Abruption also warrants evaluation before digital examination.
- Use ultrasound and the institutional emergency pathway.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask, "Why can a digital exam worsen the risk?" Common error: performing an exam to obtain more data before protecting against avoidable hemorrhage. [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 11 - Vasa previa makes fetal status the immediate priority
- Bleeding with ROM plus sudden fetal bradycardia suggests fetal blood loss.
- Escalate as a fetal emergency.
- Keep maternal and fetal assessment streams distinct.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask which finding distinguishes this from maternal hemorrhage. Common error: focusing only on maternal vital signs. [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 12 - Active learning 1: sort twelve cues under time pressure
- Teams sort clinical findings: previa, abruption, or needs-more-data.
- Each team states the first action and one action to avoid.
- Defend the answer with the cue cluster.

> NOTES: Talk track: Time for 8 minutes; debrief the digital-exam rule. Common error: identifying the condition without naming the safety action. [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 13 - Break: retain the hemorrhage map
- Before break, name the cue that signals concealed bleeding.
- After break, the focus is postpartum hemorrhage and coordinated escalation.

> NOTES: Talk track: Use a one-minute retrieval question. Common error: losing the mechanism when changing pregnancy stage. [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 14 - Postpartum hemorrhage is defined by blood loss and clinical effect
- Cumulative blood loss at least 1,000 mL or loss with hypovolemia within 24 hours meets the ACOG definition.
- Blood loss over 500 mL after vaginal birth is still abnormal and needs evaluation.
- Quantified blood loss supports early recognition.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask why the 500 mL statement still matters. Common error: waiting for the 1,000 mL threshold before acting. [ACOG PB 183]

## Slide 15 - The 4 Ts organize a hemorrhage differential
| T | Cause | Example cue |
|---|---|---|
| Tone | uterine atony | boggy uterus |
| Trauma | laceration, hematoma, rupture | firm uterus with bleeding |
| Tissue | retained placenta | continued bleeding/retained fragments |
| Thrombin | coagulopathy | diffuse bleeding, abnormal labs |

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask students which T fits a boggy uterus. Common error: giving a uterotonic before considering whether the uterus is firm. [ACOG PB 183] [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 16 - A boggy uterus requires immediate action
- First nursing action: fundal massage.
- Assess blood loss, bladder, uterine response, and vital signs.
- Escalate to oxytocin and the hemorrhage protocol.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask students to state their next action if massage does not restore tone. Common error: delaying the first action while searching for a medication. [ACOG PB 183]

## Slide 17 - Uterotonic choice depends on the patient's conditions
| Medication | Key caution/contraindication |
|---|---|
| Oxytocin | first-line; monitor fluid administration |
| Methylergonovine | avoid with hypertension/preeclampsia |
| Carboprost | avoid with asthma |
| Misoprostol | fever and shivering may occur |
| Tranexamic acid | adjunct when indicated; give early |

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask: "Which medication would be unsafe in a patient with asthma?" Common error: memorizing medications without the contraindication. [ACOG PB 183] [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 18 - DIC turns bleeding into a systems emergency
- Think DIC with abruption, massive hemorrhage, or diffuse bleeding.
- Trend bleeding, hemodynamics, urine output, and laboratory results.
- Activate the institutional hemorrhage/massive-transfusion pathway when indicated.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask why a new bleeding site matters. Common error: assuming hemorrhage is only a uterine problem. [ACOG PB 183] [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 19 - QBL and a staged protocol make escalation visible
- Measure rather than estimate blood loss.
- Use hemorrhage carts, role clarity, and standardized calls.
- Debrief events to improve the next response.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask students what they would communicate in the first SBAR sentence. Common error: reporting "a lot of blood" without quantity or trajectory. [ACOG PB 183]

## Slide 20 - First action: a boggy uterus and QBL 1,200 mL
Scenario: After vaginal birth, the uterus is boggy and QBL is 1,200 mL.

A. Wait for the provider to diagnose hemorrhage.
B. Massage the fundus and activate the hemorrhage response.
C. Give methylergonovine before reviewing history.
D. Chart the estimated loss at the end of the hour.

**Answer: B.** Immediate tone intervention and escalation address the time-critical cue.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask for the next assessment while massage is performed. Common error: selecting a medication before checking contraindications. [ACOG PB 183]

## Slide 21 - The incorrect options fail for different clinical reasons
- A fails: delay allows ongoing loss.
- C fails: hypertension/preeclampsia may contraindicate methylergonovine.
- D fails: QBL must inform real-time action.
- Transferable principle: act on the mechanism while gathering linked data.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask students to identify the 4 T that is most likely. Common error: replacing urgent action with documentation. [ACOG PB 183] [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 22 - Active learning 2: escalation drill and SBAR
- Scenario: boggy uterus, increasing QBL, tachycardia.
- Teams verbalize actions in sequence.
- One member delivers an SBAR call.
- Debrief role clarity and medication contraindications.

> NOTES: Talk track: Keep the drill to 10 minutes and replay the first minute. Common error: omitting the blood-loss trend from the handoff. [ACOG PB 183]

## Slide 23 - Loss care preserves autonomy and dignity
- Give accurate information, options, symptom care, and emotional support.
- Use the patient's preferred language for the pregnancy and loss.
- Offer support without forcing decisions or rituals.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask for one phrase that communicates non-abandonment. Common error: rushing through grief care because the emergency is over. [ACOG PB 200] [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 25 - The hemorrhage pathway links every topic today
- Early bleeding: identify instability.
- Previa/abruption: protect against the wrong examination.
- Postpartum hemorrhage: identify the 4 Ts and act.

## Slide 29 - Team roles reduce delay during hemorrhage
- Primary nurse: tone, QBL, focused assessment.
- Charge/resource nurse: activate protocol and assign roles.
- Provider/anesthesia/blood bank: parallel escalation.
- Recorder: time-stamp actions and trends.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask each student to state what information their role must communicate. Common error: several people perform the same task while another task is missed. [ACOG PB 183]

## Slide 30 - A useful handoff contains trajectory, not just a label
- Gestational/postpartum timing and mechanism suspected.
- QBL trend, uterine tone, vital signs, and response.
- Actions already taken and medication contraindications.
- Specific request for the next action.

> NOTES: Talk track: Have students say an SBAR sentence aloud. Common error: saying "PPH" without supporting data. [ACOG PB 183] [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 31 - Reassessment distinguishes a completed action from a successful action
- Did uterine tone improve?
- Is bleeding slowing or accelerating?
- Are vital signs and urine output changing?
- Does the team need to move to the next protocol stage?

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask, "What would tell you the current plan is failing?" Common error: stopping surveillance after the first intervention. [ACOG PB 183]

## Slide 32 - NCJMM transfer: cue -> mechanism -> action -> evaluation
- Recognize: bleeding pattern, uterus, vital signs.
- Analyze: pregnancy stage and likely mechanism.
- Prioritize: instability and actions to avoid.
- Take action: protocol, medication, team response.
- Evaluate: QBL and physiologic response.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask students to map the warm-up scenario. Common error: naming a condition without committing to a next action. [Dickison et al. 2019] [ACOG PB 183]
