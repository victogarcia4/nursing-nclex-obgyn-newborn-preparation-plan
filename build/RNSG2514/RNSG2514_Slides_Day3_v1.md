## Slide 1 - Time buys fetal benefit; it does not stop labor forever
- Day 3: preterm labor, prelabor ROM, corticosteroids, induction, oxytocin, and labor arrest.
- Organizing pathways: hypoxia and infection.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask, "What is the goal of tocolysis?" Common error: assuming it is intended to stop labor indefinitely. [ACOG PB 171] [ACOG CO 713]

## Slide 2 - These objectives define today's work
1. Recognize preterm-labor risk and urgent cues.
2. Explain corticosteroid timing and neuroprotection priorities.
3. Differentiate prelabor ROM from PPROM and prioritize infection prevention.
4. Deliver induction/oxytocin care safely.
5. Analyze labor patterns using the 5 Ps and current labor-arrest criteria.

## Slide 5 - Preterm birth risk is clinical and structural
- Prior spontaneous preterm birth, short cervix, multiples, uterine anomaly, infection, and short interpregnancy interval increase risk.
- Access barriers and structural inequity also shape outcomes.
- Teach risk without assigning blame.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask students to name one modifiable support need. Common error: teaching race as biological causation instead of a marker of inequity and access. [ACOG PB 234]

## Slide 6 - Preterm-labor assessment separates contractions from a changing cervix
- Assess contraction pattern, cervical change, bleeding, ROM, and fetal status.
- Cervical length and fetal fibronectin can inform risk in selected contexts.
- Fetal fibronectin is especially useful for its negative predictive value.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask what data are missing before calling a patient in preterm labor. Common error: treating uterine irritability as diagnostic. [ACOG PB 171] [ACOG PB 234]

## Slide 8 - Tocolysis buys time for specific benefits
| Agent | Key monitoring/limit |
|---|---|
| nifedipine | hypotension, headache |
| indomethacin | below 32 weeks; limited duration |
| terbutaline | tachycardia, hyperglycemia; not prolonged use |
| magnesium sulfate | fetal neuroprotection below 32 weeks, not primary tocolysis |

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask which therapy supports fetal neuroprotection. Common error: calling magnesium a routine long-term tocolytic. [ACOG PB 171] [ACOG PB 234]

## Slide 9 - Antenatal corticosteroids have a gestational-age and timing window
- A single course is recommended from 24 0/7 to 33 6/7 weeks when preterm birth within 7 days is likely.
- It may be considered from 23 0/7 weeks based on resuscitation decisions.
- It may be considered from 34 0/7 to 36 6/7 weeks for selected patients without a prior course.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask why the expected time to birth matters. Common error: giving steroids without linking them to the preterm-birth window. [ACOG CO 713]

## Slide 10 - PROM and PPROM differ by gestational age
| Condition | Definition | Primary concern |
|---|---|---|
| prelabor ROM | rupture before labor at/after 37 weeks | infection and birth planning |
| PPROM | rupture before labor before 37 weeks | infection, prematurity, cord risk |

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask students to connect the definition to management priorities. Common error: using outdated "premature ROM" language. [ACOG PB 217]

## Slide 11 - Sterile speculum assessment protects the latency period
- Use pooling, nitrazine, and ferning in the diagnostic frame.
- Minimize digital examinations after ROM.
- Assess fluid color/odor/amount, temperature, pulse, FHR, tenderness, gestational age, and position.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask, "Why do digital exams matter here?" Common error: repeating an examination out of habit. [ACOG PB 217]

## Slide 12 - PPROM requires surveillance for infection and hypoxia pathways
- Watch for chorioamnionitis, abruption, cord prolapse, oligohydramnios, cord compression, and preterm birth.
- Vaginal bleeding with ROM is an abruption cue.
- Use latency antibiotics and corticosteroids within the indicated clinical plan.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask which new cue requires immediate escalation. Common error: focusing on fluid leakage without maternal/fetal surveillance. [ACOG PB 217] [VERIFY: local latency-antibiotic regimen]

## Slide 13 - Active learning 1: order the PPROM first five actions
- Scenario: 30 weeks, gush of fluid, fluid pooling, no active bleeding.
- Teams order assessments and precautions.
- Explain why a digital exam is not among the first actions.

> NOTES: Talk track: Give 8 minutes and require a safety rationale. Common error: treating the diagnosis as sufficient without action ordering. [ACOG PB 217]

## Slide 14 - Break: keep the infection and hypoxia pathways visible
- ROM raises infection, cord, abruption, and preterm-birth concerns.
- After break, use the same safety logic for induction and oxytocin.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask students to name the first cord-prolapse risk cue. Common error: forgetting fetal position assessment. [ACOG PB 217]

## Slide 15 - Induction starts with indication, contraindication, and readiness
- Indications include postterm pregnancy, prelabor ROM, hypertensive disease, FGR, diabetes, chorioamnionitis, and IUFD in appropriate contexts.
- Contraindications include placenta/vasa previa, transverse lie, prior classical incision, active genital herpes, and cord prolapse.
- Assess cervical readiness with the Bishop score.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask which condition makes induction unsafe. Common error: treating an indication as a reason to ignore a contraindication. [ACOG-SMFM OCC 1] [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 16 - Bishop score guides cervical-ripening planning
- A low score signals lower likelihood of successful induction.
- Mechanical and pharmacologic ripening methods may be considered.
- The score is one input, not a guarantee.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask what the score changes in the plan. Common error: using a score to predict a patient's outcome with certainty. [ACOG-SMFM OCC 1]

## Slide 17 - Oxytocin is a high-alert medication linked to fetal response
- Use a pump and secondary line according to protocol.
- Titrate to contraction pattern and fetal response, not a target dose.
- Maintain continuous EFM and assess for tachysystole.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask what data must be documented with every change. Common error: treating the dose as the goal rather than a means. [ACOG-SMFM OCC 1]

## Slide 18 - Tachysystole requires an intrauterine-resuscitation sequence
- Reduce or stop oxytocin.
- Reposition left lateral and give IV fluid bolus as ordered.
- Consider terbutaline per protocol and notify the provider.
- Reassess contraction pattern and FHR response.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask why current ACOG guidance recommends against routine maternal oxygen for Category II/III without maternal hypoxia. Common error: continuing oxytocin while waiting for the provider. [ACOG 2025 CPG] [ACOG-SMFM OCC 1]

## Slide 19 - Labor progress is interpreted through the 5 Ps
| P | Examples |
|---|---|
| Passenger | size, presentation, position |
| Passageway | pelvis, soft tissue |
| Powers | strength and coordination |
| Position | maternal movement/position |
| Psyche | fear, exhaustion, unrelieved pain |

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask which P a peanut ball may influence. Common error: treating psyche as nonclinical. [ACOG-SMFM OCC 1] [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 20 - Slow but progressive labor is not labor arrest
- Contemporary criteria allow more time than historic Friedman-curve expectations.
- Adequate time and context matter before diagnosing labor arrest.
- Use current institutional criteria before labeling induction failure or arrest.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask why a premature label can cause harm. Common error: using "failure to progress" instead of current labor-arrest terminology. [ACOG-SMFM OCC 1] [VERIFY: institutional criteria]

## Slide 21 - First action: tachysystole during oxytocin infusion
Scenario: Contractions are occurring every 1 minute with recurrent late decelerations while oxytocin infuses.

A. Increase oxytocin to improve progress.
B. Stop/reduce oxytocin, reposition, give IV fluid as ordered, and notify provider.
C. Perform a routine digital exam before changing the infusion.
D. Give routine maternal oxygen despite normal maternal oxygenation.

**Answer: B.** It addresses uterine activity and fetal response first.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask which actions occur in parallel. Common error: selecting oxygen as a reflex when maternal hypoxia is absent. [ACOG 2025 CPG] [ACOG-SMFM OCC 1]

## Slide 22 - The other options fail because they worsen or delay the mechanism
- A fails: more oxytocin can worsen tachysystole.
- C fails: the infusion and fetal response demand immediate action.
- D fails: current ACOG guidance recommends against routine oxygen absent maternal hypoxia.
- Transferable principle: remove the reversible driver and reassess.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask students to state the document currency flag. Common error: defending a legacy practice without checking current guidance. [ACOG 2025 CPG] [VERIFY: document number]

## Slide 24 - Preterm labor and induction both demand realistic goals
- Tocolysis buys time for fetal benefit.
- Corticosteroids depend on gestational age and anticipated birth timing.
- Induction requires readiness, safety, and patience.
- Labor arrest is a diagnosis after adequate assessment, not a synonym for slow labor.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask students to give one non-negotiable safety rule from each topic. Common error: replacing individualized assessment with a rigid timeline. [ACOG CO 713] [ACOG-SMFM OCC 1]

## Slide 29 - A closed-loop response protects the fetus and the patient
- Identify the time-sensitive cue.
- Remove the reversible driver.
- Communicate the trend and request help.
- Reassess both uterine activity and fetal response.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask students to apply this loop to both PPROM and tachysystole. Common error: treating intervention as complete without evaluation. [ACOG PB 217] [ACOG 2025 CPG]

## Slide 30 - One comparison table can prevent a high-stakes mix-up
| Cue | Preterm labor | PPROM | Tachysystole |
|---|---|---|---|
| Core data | contractions plus cervical change | fluid/ROM confirmation | contraction frequency plus FHR |
| First safety focus | fetal maturity/transfer | infection and cord risk | remove oxytocin driver |
| Avoid | assuming contractions prove labor | repeated digital exams | continued titration |

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask what patient-specific data would modify each row. Common error: forcing all three conditions into one pathway. [ACOG PB 171] [ACOG PB 217] [ACOG-SMFM OCC 1]

## Slide 31 - Patient teaching should translate the urgency
- Report fluid leakage, bleeding, fever, contractions, and decreased fetal movement promptly.
- Explain what tests and precautions are for.
- Provide a direct pathway for calling or returning.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask students to write a one-sentence PPROM discharge warning. Common error: using technical terms without action language. [ACOG PB 217] [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 32 - NCJMM transfer: preserve time for the right intervention
- Recognize: ROM, contraction, FHR, infection, and labor-pattern cues.
- Analyze: gestational age, cervical change, and fetal response.
- Prioritize: hypoxia/infection and medication-driven risk.
- Act and evaluate with a team.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask students to name one evaluation parameter after each intervention. Common error: defining the condition without naming outcome measures. [Dickison et al. 2019] [ACOG PB 217]

## Slide 33 - Final synthesis: current terminology protects current care
- Use prelabor ROM and PPROM.

- Use Category II/III terminology and current oxygen guidance.
