**Patient:** Sofía, 30, G2 P1, at 36 weeks, presents reporting absent fetal movement for a day.

### Scene 1 — Recognize Cues (highlight)
**Chart:** Reports no fetal movement for ~24 hours; no fetal heart tones on Doppler; ultrasound confirms absent cardiac activity; patient tearful, asking what happens next.
**Item.** Highlight the cues establishing intrauterine fetal demise and the family's immediate needs.
**Key:** absent movement + absent cardiac activity on ultrasound (confirms IUFD) + acute grief and need for information.
**Rationale.** Ultrasound-confirmed absent cardiac activity establishes IUFD; the psychosocial need is now central [ACOG OCC No. 10].

### Scene 2 — Analyze Cues (matrix)
**Item.** For each nursing consideration, mark *Priority now* or *Later/secondary*.

| Consideration | Answer |
|---|---|
| Compassionate, clear communication | Priority now |
| Assessing maternal coagulation status over time | Priority now (medical safety) |
| Offering choices for seeing/holding the infant | Priority now |
| Detailed contraception counseling | Later/secondary |

**Rationale.** Emotional support and maternal safety (prolonged retention risks coagulopathy) come first; routine counseling waits [ACOG OCC No. 10].

### Scene 3 — Prioritize Hypotheses (cloze/dropdown)
**Item.** The nursing priority is **[compassionate bereavement care]** while ensuring **[maternal physical safety, including monitoring for coagulopathy]** during delivery planning.
**Rationale.** Bereavement care and maternal safety run in parallel; neither is deferred [ACOG OCC No. 10].

### Scene 4 — Generate Solutions (extended multiple response)
**Item.** Select the 3 appropriate supportive actions.
- ✅ Offer, without pressure, the option to see, hold, name, and create keepsakes
- ✅ Ask about and honor cultural and spiritual practices
- ✅ Provide clear information about the birth process and choices
- ❌ Tell her she can "have another baby"
- ❌ Discourage seeing the infant to "protect" her
- ❌ Rush decisions to move things along

**Rationale.** Follow the family's lead; support meaning-making rather than steering grief [ACOG OCC No. 10].

### Scene 5 — Take Actions (SATA)
**Item.** Which nursing actions provide sensitive, safe care? Select all that apply.
- ✅ Provide a private, unhurried environment
- ✅ Offer bereavement resources and spiritual care
- ✅ Monitor maternal vital signs and labs per plan
- ✅ Document the family's wishes and the care provided
- ❌ Make decisions for the family about memory-making

**Rationale.** Support autonomy and dignity while maintaining medical monitoring [ACOG OCC No. 10].

### Scene 6 — Evaluate Outcomes (trend)
**Over the stay:** Admission — acute shock and questions; after support and choices offered — family creates keepsakes and involves clergy; before discharge — family verbalizes feeling respected and has follow-up bereavement and medical appointments.
**Item.** Which interpretation best evaluates the care?
**Key:** Care honored the family's wishes, maintained maternal safety, and connected them to follow-up — patient-centered bereavement goals were met.
**Rationale.** Evaluate bereavement care by whether the family felt supported and their choices were honored, alongside maternal safety [ACOG OCC No. 10].

---

## Debrief — three decision points students most often miss
1. **Minimizing the loss.** "You can have another" and similar phrases invalidate grief; acknowledge this baby.
2. **Deciding for the family.** Seeing/holding and memory-making are offered, never imposed or withheld.
3. **Forgetting maternal safety.** Prolonged fetal retention risks coagulopathy; monitoring continues amid the emotional care.
