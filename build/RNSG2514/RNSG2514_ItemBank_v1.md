## 2514-D1-101 — standalone_mc · Analyze Cues · moderate
*Objective:* 2514-D1-3 · *Client Needs:* Reduction of Risk Potential · *Source:* [Lowdermilk 13th ed.]

**Stem.** A patient at 34 weeks reports a sudden gush of bright-red vaginal blood. She has no pain, and the uterus is soft and non-tender. Fetal heart rate is 140 with moderate variability. Which finding pattern does the nurse recognize?

**Key.** ["Placenta previa"]

**Rationale (correct).** Painless, bright-red bleeding with a soft, non-tender uterus and reassuring fetal status is the classic previa pattern. The transferable principle: in late bleeding, pain and uterine tone separate previa from abruption before any imaging.

**Distractor rationales.**
- *Abruptio placentae:* Right for painful, dark bleeding with a rigid, tender uterus and often a Category II/III tracing.
- *Uterine rupture:* Right when there is loss of station, acute pain, and fetal bradycardia, usually during TOLAC.
- *Ruptured vasa previa:* Right when membrane rupture is followed by bleeding plus sudden fetal bradycardia — the blood is fetal.

## 2514-D1-102 — standalone_mc · Take Actions · moderate
*Objective:* 2514-D1-3 · *Client Needs:* Reduction of Risk Potential · *Source:* [Lowdermilk 13th ed.]

**Stem.** A patient at 32 weeks arrives with painless bright-red vaginal bleeding. Placental location is not yet documented. Which action should the nurse take first?

**Key.** ["Withhold digital vaginal examination and prepare for ultrasound"]

**Rationale (correct).** Until previa is excluded by ultrasound, a digital exam can provoke catastrophic hemorrhage. The principle: with undiagnosed late-pregnancy bleeding, imaging precedes any vaginal exam.

**Distractor rationales.**
- *Perform a digital cervical exam to assess dilation:* Right in normal labor once previa is excluded — here it is contraindicated.
- *Insert a urinary catheter and begin oxytocin:* Right for augmenting labor, not for undiagnosed APH; oxytocin does not address bleeding source.
- *Place the patient in Trendelenburg position:* Not evidence-based for obstetric bleeding and can impair respiratory effort.

## 2514-D1-104 — standalone_mc · Recognize Cues · easy
*Objective:* 2514-D1-1 · *Client Needs:* Physiological Adaptation · *Source:* [Lowdermilk 13th ed.]

**Stem.** A patient at 9 weeks reports heavy bleeding and passage of grape-like vesicular tissue. Serum hCG is markedly higher than expected for dates and no fetal heart tones are found. Which condition do these findings suggest?

**Key.** ["Gestational trophoblastic disease (molar pregnancy)"]

**Rationale (correct).** Vesicular tissue, disproportionately high hCG, and absent fetal heart tones define a molar pregnancy, which needs evacuation and serial hCG follow-up. Principle: hCG far above dates with vesicles is molar until proven otherwise.

**Distractor rationales.**
- *Threatened miscarriage:* Right for bleeding with a closed cervix and a viable intrauterine pregnancy on ultrasound.
- *Ectopic pregnancy:* Right for unilateral pain with lower-than-expected hCG and no intrauterine sac.
- *Implantation bleeding:* Right for light spotting around the expected menses with normal hCG rise.

**Key.** ["Possible ectopic pregnancy requiring close follow-up"]

**Rationale (correct).** An hCG above the discriminatory zone with no intrauterine sac raises concern for ectopic pregnancy and mandates urgent evaluation. Principle: 'no sac when hCG says there should be one' is an ectopic red flag.

**Distractor rationales.**

- *Molar pregnancy:* Right with vesicular tissue and hCG far higher than expected for dates.

**Stem.** Ten minutes after a vaginal birth, the uterus is boggy and above the umbilicus, and there is a steady flow of blood. Select the 2 immediate actions, the 1 condition, and the 2 parameters to monitor.

**Key.** {"actions": ["Firmly massage the fundus", "Administer oxytocin per protocol"], "condition": ["Uterine atony"], "parameters": ["Fundal tone and position", "Quantified blood loss and vital signs"]}

**Rationale (correct).** A boggy, high uterus with bleeding is atony — the first of the 4 Ts and the leading PPH cause. Massage plus oxytocin are first-line; tone and quantified loss track the response. Principle: treat tone first because atony causes most PPH.

- *Deep tendon reflexes:* Right to monitor on magnesium therapy, not for hemorrhage.

## 2514-D1-109 — standalone_mc · Analyze Cues · hard
*Objective:* 2514-D1-4 · *Client Needs:* Physiological Adaptation · *Source:* [ACOG PB 183 (2017)]

**Stem.** A postpartum patient has oozing from her IV site and gums, bruising, and continued vaginal bleeding. Fibrinogen is low and platelets are falling. Which process do these findings indicate?

**Key.** ["Disseminated intravascular coagulation"]

**Rationale (correct).** Bleeding from multiple sites with falling fibrinogen and platelets is consumptive coagulopathy (DIC), often triggered by abruption, HELLP, or massive PPH. Principle: oozing from many sites means the clotting system itself has failed.

**Distractor rationales.**
- *Normal postpartum involution:* Right for a firm fundus with decreasing lochia and stable labs.
- *Isolated vaginal laceration:* Right for localized bright bleeding from a visualized tear with normal coagulation.
- *Von Willebrand carrier state at baseline:* Right as a chronic history clue, but does not explain acutely falling fibrinogen and platelets.

## 2514-D1-110 — standalone_mc · Take Actions · moderate
*Objective:* 2514-D1-5 · *Client Needs:* Pharmacological and Parenteral Therapies · *Source:* [ACOG PB 181 (2017)]

**Stem.** An Rh-negative patient experiences a first-trimester pregnancy loss with bleeding. Her antibody screen is negative. Which nursing action is indicated?

**Key.** ["Administer Rh(D) immune globulin as ordered"]

**Rationale (correct).** Bleeding or loss in an Rh-negative, unsensitized patient is an indication for Rh(D) immune globulin to prevent alloimmunization. Principle: any fetomaternal bleeding event in an Rh-negative patient triggers anti-D.

**Distractor rationales.**
- *No intervention is needed because it was early:* Sensitization can occur even in early loss; 'early' does not remove the indication.
- *Administer the rubella vaccine now:* Right to offer rubella-non-immune patients postpartum, unrelated to Rh status.
- *Schedule the anti-D only if a future pregnancy occurs:* Too late — prophylaxis must be given at the sensitizing event.

**Stem.** A patient at 33 weeks with no prior hypertension has a blood pressure of 148/94 on two readings 4 hours apart and a urine protein-to-creatinine ratio of 0.2. Which classification do the findings support?

**Key.** ["Gestational hypertension"]

**Rationale (correct).** New hypertension after 20 weeks without significant proteinuria (P:C < 0.3) or severe features is gestational hypertension. Principle: timing after 20 weeks plus absence of proteinuria separates gHTN from chronic HTN and preeclampsia.

**Distractor rationales.**
- *Chronic hypertension:* Right when hypertension predates pregnancy or appears before 20 weeks.
- *Preeclampsia with severe features:* Right with severe-range BP or end-organ signs, absent here.
- *Normal pregnancy blood pressure:* Incorrect — 148/94 exceeds the 140/90 threshold.

## 2514-D2-203 — standalone_mc · Recognize Cues · hard
*Objective:* 2514-D2-2 · *Client Needs:* Reduction of Risk Potential · *Source:* [ACOG PB 222 (2020)]

**Stem.** A patient at 37 weeks has a blood pressure of 162/110 and platelets of 84,000 but a urine protein-to-creatinine ratio of 0.1. Which statement best reflects the diagnostic reasoning?

**Key.** ["Preeclampsia with severe features can be diagnosed without proteinuria"]

**Rationale (correct).** Since 2013, preeclampsia can be diagnosed without proteinuria when severe-range BP plus a severe feature (here, thrombocytopenia) are present. Principle: absence of proteinuria never rules out preeclampsia.

**Distractor rationales.**
- *Preeclampsia is excluded because proteinuria is absent:* Reflects the outdated rule that proteinuria is required.
- *This is gestational hypertension only:* Right only if there were no proteinuria AND no severe features; a severe feature is present.
- *This requires a 24-hour urine before any diagnosis:* Right as a quantification method in some cases, but it is not required to act on severe features.

**Stem.** A patient receiving IV magnesium sulfate for seizure prophylaxis has a respiratory rate of 10, absent patellar reflexes, and urine output of 20 mL over the last 2 hours. Which action is the priority?

**Key.** ["Stop the magnesium infusion and prepare calcium gluconate"]

**Rationale (correct).** Loss of reflexes with respiratory depression signals magnesium toxicity; stop the infusion and give the antidote, calcium gluconate. Principle: reflexes disappear before respiratory and cardiac depression — treat at the first toxicity sign.

**Distractor rationales.**
- *Increase the magnesium infusion rate:* Right only when reflexes are present and levels are subtherapeutic — dangerous here.
- *Administer an additional loading dose:* Correct at initiation of therapy, not during toxicity.
- *Document and reassess in 1 hour:* Delays antidote during a life-threatening trend.
