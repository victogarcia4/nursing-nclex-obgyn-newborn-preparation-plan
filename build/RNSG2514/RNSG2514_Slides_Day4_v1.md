## Slide 4 - Warm-up: choose the first action for a visible cord
- ROM is followed by sudden prolonged deceleration.
- A cord is visible at the introitus.
- What must happen immediately before transport or documentation?

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask students to state the first two actions in order. Common error: attempting to replace the cord. [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 5 - Operative vaginal birth has prerequisites, not just indications
- Fully dilated cervix, ruptured membranes, engaged head with known position, adequate anesthesia, empty bladder, consent, and readiness to abandon attempt.
- Forceps and vacuum have distinct maternal and neonatal risks.
- Nursing care includes preparation, surveillance, and communication.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask which missing prerequisite should stop the attempt. Common error: treating operative birth as a shortcut when position is not known. [ACOG PB 219] [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 6 - Cesarean care begins before incision and continues after birth
- Verify consent, prophylaxis timing, thromboprophylaxis, and left uterine displacement.
- Monitor hemorrhage, infection, pain, mobility, breastfeeding, and emotional response.
- An unplanned surgical birth may require additional support.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask for one postoperative cue that requires escalation. Common error: ending nursing attention at intraoperative preparation. [ACOG PB 219] [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 7 - FGR is the current clinical term for restricted fetal growth
- Sonographic estimated fetal weight or abdominal circumference below the 10th percentile defines FGR.
- Symmetric patterns suggest early insult; asymmetric patterns suggest later uteroplacental insufficiency.
- Etiology guides surveillance and timing decisions.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask which data are needed before explaining an FGR pattern. Common error: using older terminology in clinical teaching. [ACOG PB 227]

## Slide 8 - Umbilical-artery Doppler changes the urgency of surveillance
- Serial growth, amniotic-fluid assessment, and Doppler studies guide follow-up.
- Absent or reversed end-diastolic flow signals severe compromise.
- Support appointment adherence, transportation, work-leave, and patient understanding.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask why adherence barriers are a nursing concern. Common error: treating a missed scan as simple noncompliance. [ACOG PB 227]

## Slide 9 - Uterine rupture is a cluster diagnosis with an early FHR cue
- Abnormal FHR, often prolonged deceleration or bradycardia, is a common early sign.
- Other cues: sudden pain, loss of station, cessation of contractions, bleeding, maternal instability.
- Activate emergency cesarean and parallel resuscitation.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask which cue is most likely to appear first. Common error: waiting for all classic signs before escalating. [ACOG PB 205] [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 10 - Uterine rupture response is a multidisciplinary emergency
- Call for help and activate emergency cesarean response.
- Support large-bore access, blood-bank notification, positioning, and family communication.
- Prepare for hemorrhage and possible hysterectomy.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask which tasks can occur in parallel. Common error: delaying team activation while collecting every detail. [ACOG PB 205] [Lowdermilk] [VERIFY: local decision-to-incision standards]

## Slide 11 - FHR categories describe urgency, not a diagnosis by themselves
| Category | Interpretation | Action frame |
|---|---|---|
| I | normal | continue surveillance |
| II | indeterminate | evaluate, correct reversible causes, reassess |
| III | abnormal | resuscitate and expedite if unresolved |

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask which category contains the majority of tracings. Common error: treating Category II as a guaranteed emergency cesarean. [ACOG 2025 CPG] [Macones et al. 2008]

## Slide 12 - Variable decelerations point to cord compression
- VEAL CHOP: Variable -> Cord; Early -> Head; Accelerations -> Okay; Late -> Placental insufficiency.
- Treat the tracing in the clinical context.
- Use position change, fluid, oxytocin reduction, amnioinfusion when indicated, and correction of maternal causes.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask which intervention removes a reversible driver during oxytocin use. Common error: using a mnemonic as a complete management plan. [ACOG 2025 CPG] [Macones et al. 2008]

## Slide 13 - Routine maternal oxygen is not recommended for Category II/III without maternal hypoxia
- Current ACOG guidance recommends against routine oxygen in the absence of maternal hypoxia.
- Correct reversible maternal or uterine causes instead.
- The guideline document number requires verification before printing.

> NOTES: Talk track: Name this as a reversal from legacy teaching. Common error: adding oxygen automatically while continuing an avoidable driver. [ACOG 2025 CPG] [VERIFY: document number]

## Slide 14 - Active learning 1: the cord-prolapse sequence must be automatic
- Call for help.
- Manually elevate the presenting part and keep the hand in place until delivery.
- Position knee-chest, Trendelenburg, or exaggerated Sims.
- Keep visible cord moist; do not replace it; prepare immediate cesarean.

> NOTES: Talk track: Run a 3-minute timed drill, then debrief the first 30 seconds. Common error: releasing elevation during transport. [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 15 - Break: keep hypoxia at the center of the response
- FHR pattern, cord status, maternal condition, and uterine activity guide parallel action.
- After break, turn to neonatal anticipation and bereavement care.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask what response is contraindicated when a cord is visible. Common error: attempting cord replacement. [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 16 - Anticipate neonatal needs before the birth occurs
- Every complication may change who should attend the birth.
- Initial NRP assessment: term? tone? breathing/crying?
- Warm, dry, stimulate, and position airway as indicated.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask which complications today require neonatal-team anticipation. Common error: waiting for poor transition before requesting help. [Weiner & Zaichkin 2021] [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 17 - Positive-pressure ventilation is the key NRP intervention for apnea or bradycardia
- PPV is the most important intervention for an apneic or bradycardic newborn.
- Escalate to compressions and epinephrine according to the algorithm.
- Apgar describes response; it does not direct resuscitation or diagnose asphyxia.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask students to separate a descriptor from a treatment algorithm. Common error: waiting for an Apgar score before beginning resuscitation. [Weiner & Zaichkin 2021] [VERIFY: current NRP edition]

## Slide 18 - Compromised newborn risks follow the prenatal pathway
| Antepartum/intrapartum cue | Neonatal anticipation |
|---|---|
| diabetes or FGR | hypoglycemia, temperature support |
| FHR compromise/rupture | acid-base status and resuscitation readiness |
| meconium or preterm birth | respiratory support readiness |
| infection risk | sepsis evaluation pathway |

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask how the neonatal team should be briefed before birth. Common error: giving a generic handoff without the pregnancy complication. [Weiner & Zaichkin 2021] [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 19 - Bereavement care is active nursing care, not an afterthought
- Follow the family's lead on seeing and holding the baby; offer more than once without insistence.
- Offer memory-making options and use the baby's name.
- Avoid minimizing phrases; allow silence.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask students to practice one supportive phrase. Common error: saying "at least" or immediately focusing on a future pregnancy. [ACOG OCC 10]

## Slide 20 - Cultural and spiritual questions invite, not assume
- Ask about rites, burial/cremation practices, family presence, and who should be told.
- Offer chaplaincy, interpreter support, and materials in the preferred language.
- Discuss lactation support because milk production may begin.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask what a respectful open-ended question sounds like. Common error: assuming a family's beliefs from identity or religion. [ACOG OCC 10] [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 21 - First action: visible cord with fetal bradycardia
Scenario: After ROM, a visible cord appears and the FHR falls to 70/min.

A. Attempt to replace the cord.
B. Manually elevate the presenting part, call for help, and position for immediate delivery.
C. Document the event before touching the patient.
D. Continue oxytocin to speed vaginal birth.

**Answer: B.** Elevation and emergency activation preserve fetal perfusion.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask what happens to the gloved hand after elevation begins. Common error: releasing pressure to complete another task. [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 22 - The other options worsen or delay the hypoxia pathway
- A fails: do not attempt to replace the cord.
- C fails: action precedes documentation in an immediate emergency.
- D fails: discontinue oxytocin and prepare emergency delivery.
- Transferable principle: relieve the mechanical cause while the team prepares definitive care.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask students to name the transport position. Common error: treating the FHR decline as a reason to wait for more monitoring. [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 23 - Active learning 2: bereavement communication practice
- Practice a phrase to use, a phrase to avoid, and an open-ended cultural/spiritual question.
- Debrief how silence and presence can be therapeutic.
- Identify when to offer grief, mental-health, or lactation referral.

> NOTES: Talk track: Normalize discomfort while requiring respectful language. Common error: filling silence with reassurance that minimizes loss. [ACOG OCC 10]

## Slide 30 - High-acuity response still requires role clarity
- Primary nurse: relieve compression and monitor FHR.
- Resource nurse: call team, prepare transport, coordinate roles.
- Provider/OR/neonatal team: definitive response in parallel.
- Recorder/family support: document and communicate.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask where family communication belongs during the emergency. Common error: assuming emergency care makes communication optional. [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 31 - Reassessment tells the team whether resuscitation is working
- FHR response and uterine activity.
- Maternal oxygenation and hemodynamics.
- New bleeding, loss of station, or neonatal transition status.
- Family understanding and emotional support needs after the event.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask for one reassessment measure per pathway. Common error: stopping surveillance after a single improved tracing. [ACOG 2025 CPG] [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 32 - Comparison: reversible FHR causes versus immediate delivery cues
| Situation | Initial focus | Definitive direction |
|---|---|---|
| Category II with reversible driver | correct cause; reassess | continue if response returns |
| Category III unresolved | resuscitation | expedite delivery |
| cord prolapse | elevate presenting part | immediate delivery preparation |
| rupture cluster | activate emergency response | emergency cesarean |

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask why cord prolapse has a different mechanical action. Common error: treating all FHR abnormalities with the same sequence. [ACOG 2025 CPG] [ACOG PB 205] [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 33 - NCJMM transfer: identify the pathway, then protect it
- Recognize FHR, bleeding, station, and neonatal cues.
- Analyze the likely hemorrhage, hypertension, or hypoxia pathway.
- Prioritize the threat that cannot wait.
- Act in parallel and evaluate outcome.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask students to map cord prolapse and bereavement care to different NCJMM operations. Common error: equating clinical judgment only with technical emergency actions. [Dickison et al. 2019] [ACOG OCC 10]

## Slide 34 - Final synthesis: precision and compassion coexist
- Use current terminology and current FHR guidance.
- Rehearse the cord-prolapse sequence until automatic.
- Anticipate neonatal needs before birth.
- Let bereavement care reflect the family's values and choices.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask students to name the action they will carry into clinical. Common error: assuming empathy takes time away from safe care. [ACOG 2025 CPG] [ACOG OCC 10]
