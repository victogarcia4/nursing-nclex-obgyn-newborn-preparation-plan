# Unfolding Case (WO-G) — Rh-Negative Client Across Pregnancy
**Course:** RNSG 1523 Nursing I · **Traces to objectives:** 1523-D3-5, 1523-D2-4 · **Format:** 6 scenes, one per NCJMM operation.

**Patient:** Aisha, 24, G1 P0, first prenatal visit at 10 weeks. Blood type **O negative**.

---

### Scene 1 — Recognize Cues (matrix/highlight)
**Chart (10 weeks):** Blood type O negative; antibody screen **negative**; partner's blood type reported Rh-positive.
**Item.** Highlight the findings that establish this patient's alloimmunization risk.
**Key:** maternal Rh-negative status + negative antibody screen (unsensitized) + partner Rh-positive.
**Rationale.** An unsensitized Rh-negative patient carrying a potentially Rh-positive fetus is at risk for alloimmunization [ACOG PB 181].

### Scene 2 — Analyze Cues (matrix)
**Item.** For each event, mark whether it *Is* or *Is not* a potential sensitizing event.

| Event | Answer |
|---|---|
| First-trimester bleeding | Is a sensitizing event |
| Amniocentesis | Is a sensitizing event |
| Routine reaching 28 weeks | Is a scheduled prophylaxis point |
| A normal prenatal BP check | Is not a sensitizing event |

**Rationale.** Fetomaternal bleeding from procedures, bleeding, or birth can sensitize; routine anti-D is also given at 28 weeks [ACOG PB 181].

### Scene 3 — Prioritize Hypotheses (cloze/dropdown)
**Item.** For this unsensitized Rh-negative patient, the priority preventive measure is **[Rh(D) immune globulin]**, given to prevent **[maternal anti-D antibody formation]** that could harm a future Rh-positive fetus.
**Rationale.** Anti-D prophylaxis prevents sensitization; it is prevention, not treatment [ACOG PB 181].

### Scene 4 — Generate Solutions (extended multiple response)
**Item.** Select the 3 correct elements of the anti-D plan across this pregnancy.
- ✅ Administer Rh(D) immune globulin routinely at about 28 weeks
- ✅ Administer anti-D after any sensitizing event (bleeding, amniocentesis)
- ✅ Administer anti-D within 72 hours postpartum if the newborn is Rh-positive
- ❌ Give anti-D only if she develops symptoms
- ❌ Withhold anti-D because the antibody screen is negative
- ❌ Give the rubella vaccine in place of anti-D

**Rationale.** A negative screen is exactly why she still needs prophylaxis; it means she is not yet sensitized [ACOG PB 181].

### Scene 5 — Take Actions (drag-and-drop ordering)
**Scenario:** At 28 weeks the antibody screen is repeated and remains negative; anti-D is ordered.
**Item.** Place the steps in order.
1. Verify Rh-negative status and negative antibody screen
2. Confirm the order and educate the patient on purpose and timing
3. Administer Rh(D) immune globulin as ordered
4. Document administration and schedule postpartum reassessment

**Rationale.** Verification precedes administration; documentation closes the loop for the postpartum dose [ACOG PB 181].

### Scene 6 — Evaluate Outcomes (trend)
**Postpartum:** Newborn typed **Rh-positive**. Maternal antibody screen at delivery negative. Anti-D given at 30 hours postpartum.
**Item.** Which statement best evaluates the outcome?
**Key:** Prophylaxis was completed correctly and on time — antenatal dose at 28 weeks plus postpartum dose within 72 hours for an Rh-positive infant.
**Rationale.** Timely completion of both doses is the marker of successful prevention; the same negative screen with correct timing indicates no sensitization occurred [ACOG PB 181].

---

## Debrief — three decision points students most often miss
1. **Reading a negative antibody screen as "no anti-D needed."** The negative screen confirms she is *unsensitized* — the population that benefits from prophylaxis.
2. **Missing the 72-hour postpartum window** when the infant is Rh-positive, even after the 28-week dose.
3. **Confusing anti-D with a vaccine.** Rh(D) immune globulin is passive antibody prophylaxis, not immunization; the rubella vaccine is a separate postpartum item.
