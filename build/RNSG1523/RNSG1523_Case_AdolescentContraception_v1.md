**Patient:** Jordan, 16, presents to a school-based clinic requesting contraception. Reports occasional migraine **with aura**. No chronic conditions, nonsmoker.

### Scene 1 — Recognize Cues (highlight)
**Chart:** Age 16; migraine with aura; nonsmoker; sexually active; requests a method she does not have to take daily; no personal or family history of clotting disorder disclosed.
**Item.** Highlight the findings most relevant to method eligibility.
**Key:** migraine **with aura** and the stated preference for a non-daily method.
**Rationale.** Migraine with aura is the key estrogen-eligibility cue; the non-daily preference points toward LARC [CDC US MEC].

### Scene 2 — Analyze Cues (matrix)
**Item.** For each method, mark *Generally appropriate* vs *Estrogen concern — avoid combined*.

| Method | Answer |
|---|---|
| Combined oral contraceptive | Estrogen concern — avoid combined |
| Levonorgestrel IUD | Generally appropriate |
| Etonogestrel implant | Generally appropriate |
| Progestin-only pill | Generally appropriate |

**Rationale.** Migraine with aura makes estrogen-containing methods a higher stroke risk; progestin-only and non-hormonal or LARC options remain appropriate [CDC US MEC].

### Scene 3 — Prioritize Hypotheses (cloze/dropdown)
**Item.** Given her aura history and preference, the method category to prioritize discussing is **[long-acting reversible contraception]**, while the category to avoid is **[combined estrogen-progestin methods]**.
**Rationale.** LARC matches both effectiveness and her adherence preference while sidestepping the estrogen contraindication [CDC US MEC].

### Scene 4 — Generate Solutions (extended multiple response)
**Item.** Select the 3 best counseling actions.
- ✅ Present LARC and progestin-only options with typical-use effectiveness
- ✅ Provide condom teaching for STI prevention regardless of method
- ✅ Explain confidentiality and consent within local rules `[VERIFY: Texas]`
- ❌ Insist she choose a combined pill
- ❌ Require parental presence before any discussion `[VERIFY: local law]`
- ❌ Dismiss her preference for a non-daily method

**Rationale.** Adolescent counseling pairs effective method options with dual protection and respects autonomy within the law [CDC US MEC].

### Scene 5 — Take Actions (SATA)
**Item.** Which nursing actions are appropriate at this visit? Select all that apply.
- ✅ Teach ACHES/PATCH-relevant warning signs for the chosen method
- ✅ Document education and the shared decision
- ✅ Offer STI screening and condoms
- ❌ Enter a combined pill order because it is inexpensive

**Rationale.** Method-specific danger-sign teaching and dual protection are core to safe initiation [CDC US MEC].

### Scene 6 — Evaluate Outcomes (trend)
**Follow-up over 6 months:** Month 1 — chose the levonorgestrel IUD, tolerating well; Month 3 — lighter menses, no aura-related events; Month 6 — satisfied, correct condom use reported.
**Item.** Which interpretation best evaluates the plan?
**Key:** The chosen method is effective and well tolerated, and dual protection is maintained — the counseling met her needs and avoided the estrogen risk.
**Rationale.** Evaluate contraception by continuation, tolerability, satisfaction, and STI protection over time, not a single visit [CDC US MEC].

## Debrief — three decision points students most often miss
1. **Overlooking aura.** "Migraine" alone is not the discriminator; *aura* is what restricts estrogen.
2. **Forgetting dual protection.** Any hormonal or LARC method still needs condom teaching for STIs.
3. **Assuming consent rules.** Minors' consent and confidentiality are set by state law and must be verified locally, never assumed. `[VERIFY: Texas]`
