# Unfolding Case (WO-G) — Warning-Sign Triage Call
**Course:** RNSG 1523 Nursing I · **Traces to objectives:** 1523-D4-6, 1523-D2-4 · **Format:** 6 scenes, one per NCJMM operation.

**Patient:** Tanya, 26, G1 P0, at 30 weeks, calls the prenatal triage line.

---

### Scene 1 — Recognize Cues (highlight)
**Call note:** Reports a severe headache "that won't go away," some blurred vision, mild nasal congestion, mild ankle swelling by evening, and new puffiness of her face and hands since this morning.
**Item.** Highlight the reports that are warning signs.
**Key:** severe persistent headache, blurred vision, and sudden facial/hand swelling.
**Rationale.** These cluster toward preeclampsia and require urgent evaluation; nasal congestion and evening ankle swelling are benign [ACOG PB 222; Lowdermilk].

### Scene 2 — Analyze Cues (matrix)
**Item.** Mark each report as *Warning sign* or *Normal discomfort*.

| Report | Answer |
|---|---|
| Severe persistent headache | Warning sign |
| Blurred vision | Warning sign |
| Sudden facial/hand swelling | Warning sign |
| Mild nasal congestion | Normal discomfort |
| Evening ankle swelling | Normal discomfort |

**Rationale.** Severity, suddenness, and neurologic/visual involvement separate danger from normal discomfort [Lowdermilk].

### Scene 3 — Prioritize Hypotheses (cloze/dropdown)
**Item.** The cluster most suggests **[preeclampsia]**, and the priority is to **[have her evaluated urgently for blood pressure and labs]**.
**Rationale.** The neurologic-visual-edema cluster points to a hypertensive emergency risk and drives immediate triage [ACOG PB 222].

### Scene 4 — Generate Solutions (extended multiple response)
**Item.** Select the 3 correct triage actions.
- ✅ Advise her to come in now (or go to OB triage) for BP and evaluation
- ✅ Ask about additional symptoms (RUQ pain, decreased fetal movement)
- ✅ Stay on the line and arrange safe transport
- ❌ Tell her to take acetaminophen and call back tomorrow
- ❌ Reassure her that headaches are always normal
- ❌ Advise bed rest at home without evaluation

**Rationale.** Warning-sign clusters require prompt in-person assessment, not home management [ACOG PB 222].

### Scene 5 — Take Actions (drag-and-drop ordering)
**Item.** Order the nurse's triage actions.
1. Recognize the warning-sign cluster and its urgency
2. Direct the patient to immediate evaluation and arrange transport
3. Communicate the report to the provider/OB triage
4. Document the call, advice given, and disposition

**Rationale.** Recognition drives disposition; hand-off and documentation follow [ACOG PB 222].

### Scene 6 — Evaluate Outcomes (trend)
**On arrival and over 2 hours:** BP 168/110 → after evaluation and treatment 150/96 → 138/88; labs show platelets 140,000, mild proteinuria; magnesium started for seizure prophylaxis; symptoms improving.
**Item.** Which interpretation best evaluates the triage decision?
**Key:** Urgent triage led to timely diagnosis and treatment; the BP and symptom trend improved — escalating the call was the correct action.
**Rationale.** Evaluate the triage by whether early recognition changed the trajectory; here it did [ACOG PB 222].

---

## Debrief — three decision points students most often miss
1. **Averaging danger with benign symptoms.** One or two warning signs in a list still trigger urgent evaluation, regardless of the benign items.
2. **Home-managing a warning-sign cluster.** Acetaminophen-and-call-back is unsafe for possible preeclampsia.
3. **Missing the sudden-vs-gradual edema distinction.** Evening ankle swelling is normal; sudden facial/hand swelling is not.
