# Unfolding Case (WO-G) — Prenatal Visit with Rising Blood Pressure
**Course:** RNSG 1523 Nursing I · **Traces to objectives:** 1523-D3-1, 1523-D4-6 · **Format:** 6 scenes, one per NCJMM operation.

**Patient:** Maria, 28, G2 T1 P0 A0 L1, singleton pregnancy. Prior birth at 39 weeks, no complications. Prepregnancy BP normal.

---

### Scene 1 — Recognize Cues (highlight)
**Chart (24 weeks):** BP 118/72, weight +6 lb, urine dip negative protein, no headache, reports normal fetal movement.
**Chart (28 weeks):** BP 128/82, weight +9 lb, trace protein, denies symptoms.
**Chart (32 weeks, today):** BP 142/92 repeated 142/94 four hours later; weight +12 lb; reports mild ankle swelling; denies headache or visual change.

**Item.** Highlight the findings across the three visits that should prompt further evaluation.
**Key:** the upward BP trend (118/72 → 128/82 → 142/92) and today's two readings ≥ 140/90.
**Rationale.** A rising trend crossing 140/90 after 20 weeks is the earliest cue of a hypertensive disorder [ACOG PB 222]. Mild ankle swelling alone is normal dependent edema.

### Scene 2 — Analyze Cues (matrix)
**New data:** Urine protein-to-creatinine ratio 0.15; platelets 210,000; AST 22; no headache, no visual change, reflexes 2+.
**Item.** For each finding, mark *Consistent with gestational hypertension* vs *Indicates a severe feature*.

| Finding | Answer |
|---|---|
| BP 142/94 | Consistent with gHTN |
| P:C ratio 0.15 | Consistent with gHTN (no significant proteinuria) |
| Platelets 210,000 | Consistent with gHTN |
| Normal AST, no CNS symptoms | Consistent with gHTN |

**Rationale.** New hypertension after 20 weeks without significant proteinuria or severe features is gestational hypertension [ACOG PB 222]. No severe feature is present today.

### Scene 3 — Prioritize Hypotheses (cloze/dropdown)
**Item.** The most likely working diagnosis is **[gestational hypertension]**, and the priority the nurse monitors for is **[progression to preeclampsia with severe features]**.
**Rationale.** gHTN can progress; surveillance for severe features and proteinuria drives follow-up [ACOG PB 222].

### Scene 4 — Generate Solutions (extended multiple response)
**Item.** Select the 3 appropriate plan elements.
- ✅ Teach preeclampsia warning signs (severe headache, visual change, RUQ pain, sudden facial/hand swelling)
- ✅ Arrange increased BP surveillance and lab follow-up
- ✅ Review fetal movement counting and when to call
- ❌ Start magnesium sulfate now (reserved for severe disease/seizure prophylaxis)
- ❌ Begin an ACE inhibitor (fetotoxic) [FDA PLLR]
- ❌ Discharge with no follow-up

**Rationale.** Education, surveillance, and fetal monitoring fit gHTN without severe features [ACOG PB 222].

### Scene 5 — Take Actions (SATA)
**Item.** Before Maria leaves, which actions should the nurse take? Select all that apply.
- ✅ Provide written warning-sign instructions and a call-back number
- ✅ Confirm the follow-up appointment and lab orders
- ✅ Document the BP trend and today's readings
- ❌ Tell her the swelling means she has preeclampsia (a conclusion not supported by data)

**Rationale.** Clear teaching, follow-up, and documentation operationalize the plan [ACOG PB 222].

### Scene 6 — Evaluate Outcomes (trend)
**Follow-up trend:** week 33 BP 138/88, denies symptoms; week 34 BP 144/94 with new P:C 0.4 and a persistent headache; reflexes 3+.
**Item.** Which interpretation should drive the next action?
**Key:** The picture is progressing toward preeclampsia (new significant proteinuria plus a CNS symptom); escalate evaluation now.
**Rationale.** A change in the trend — new proteinuria and a headache — signals progression and outweighs any single reassuring reading [ACOG PB 222].

---

## Debrief — three decision points students most often miss
1. **Treating a single normal reading as reassurance.** The story is in the *trend*; week 33's 138/88 does not cancel the trajectory.
2. **Waiting for proteinuria to act.** Preeclampsia can be diagnosed on severe features without proteinuria; do not gate action on a protein result [ACOG PB 222].
3. **Confusing normal edema with a warning sign.** Mild dependent ankle swelling is normal; *sudden facial/hand* swelling with CNS symptoms is not.
