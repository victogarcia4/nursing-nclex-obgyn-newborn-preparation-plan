# RNSG 1523 Nursing I - Day 2 Slide Deck
## Confirming and Dating Pregnancy; OB History

> WO-C | 140 minutes | Markdown slide deck | Version v1

## Slide 1 - Pregnancy dating begins with asking what the cue can prove
- Day 2: signs of pregnancy, GTPAL, dating, initial prenatal assessment, adaptation.
- Clinical judgment thread: recognize cues, document accurately, take action.

> NOTES: Talk track: Frame the day around evidence strength. Ask: "Which finding can only be explained by a fetus?" Common error: treating every pregnancy symptom as diagnostic. [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 2 - These five objectives define today's work
1. Classify presumptive, probable, and positive signs of pregnancy.
2. Document an obstetric history accurately using GTPAL.
3. Calculate an EDC/EDB using Naegele's rule and state its limitations.
4. Describe the components of the initial prenatal visit, including baseline labs.
5. Summarize normal maternal physiologic and psychological adaptations of the first trimester.

> NOTES: Talk track: Read the objectives verbatim. Ask students to circle the objective requiring the most calculation or documentation. Common error: confusing a patient's report with an examiner's finding. [Lowdermilk] [Silbert-Flagg]

## Slide 4 - The hook asks who perceived the finding
- Poll: nausea is presumptive, probable, or positive?
- Poll: a positive hCG is diagnostic, probable, or presumptive?
- Poll: fetal heart rate heard by Doppler is which class?
- Poll: what does a twin pregnancy do to G and L?

> NOTES: Talk track: Record reasoning before correcting. Common error: using the word "positive" in a test result to mean positive sign of pregnancy. [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 5 - Presumptive signs are experienced by the patient
- Amenorrhea, nausea/vomiting, fatigue, breast tenderness, urinary frequency, and quickening may be reported.
- These findings have nonpregnancy explanations.
- The nurse validates the experience while avoiding diagnostic overstatement.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask: "What alternative explanation could fit this finding?" Common error: calling a patient-reported symptom proof of pregnancy. [Lowdermilk] [Silbert-Flagg]

## Slide 6 - Probable signs are objective but not unique to pregnancy
- Goodell, Chadwick, and Hegar signs are examiner findings.
- Ballottement, Braxton Hicks contractions, uterine enlargement, and positive hCG are probable signs.
- Tumors and hormonal states can mimic some findings.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask students to sort findings into subjective and objective. Common error: believing objective means diagnostic. [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 7 - Positive signs are attributable only to a fetus
- Fetal heart rate heard by Doppler or auscultation.
- Fetus visualized on ultrasound.
- Fetal movement palpated by the examiner.
- These findings carry the strongest diagnostic meaning.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask: "Who perceived the movement?" Common error: classifying quickening felt by the patient as positive. [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 8 - The three sign classes are an evidence ladder
| Class | Perceiver | Examples | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Presumptive | patient | nausea, fatigue | suggests |
| Probable | examiner | hCG, cervical change | supports |
| Positive | examiner/technology | FHR, ultrasound fetus | attributable only to fetus |

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask students to explain why a positive test result can still be a probable sign. Common error: conflating test language with clinical classification. [Lowdermilk] [Silbert-Flagg]

## Slide 9 - GTPAL separates pregnancies from children
- G = total pregnancies, including the current pregnancy.
- T = births at or beyond 37 0/7 weeks.
- P = births from 20 0/7 through 36 6/7 weeks.
- A = losses before 20 weeks, spontaneous or induced.
- L = living children.

> NOTES: Talk track: Repeat: "G counts pregnancies; L counts children." Common error: counting twins as two pregnancies. [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 10 - Multiples split G and L differently
| Event | Gravida | Term/preterm/abortion | Living |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| Twin birth at 35 weeks | +1 | P +1 | +2 |
| Twin birth at term | +1 | T +1 | +2 |
| Current twin pregnancy | +1 | current pregnancy only | existing children only |

> NOTES: Talk track: Work one twin example on a whiteboard. Ask: "Which letter changes because there are two babies?" Common error: adding two to G for one twin pregnancy. [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 11 - GTPAL is a clinical safety record
- Ask gestational age at each birth or loss.
- Clarify whether a loss occurred before or after 20 weeks.
- Record living children separately from pregnancy count.
- Reconcile the patient's narrative with the structured field.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask: "What follow-up question resolves an ambiguous history?" Common error: entering a guessed value rather than clarifying. [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 12 - Active learning 1 makes GTPAL retrieval automatic
- Scenario A: current pregnancy; one term birth; one loss at 9 weeks.
- Scenario B: current pregnancy; twins born at 35 weeks; both living.
- Scenario C: two term births; one loss at 18 weeks; not currently pregnant.
- Teams show the calculation and the question that matters most.

> NOTES: Talk track: Use 60-second whiteboard rounds. Common error: forgetting that the current pregnancy is included in G. [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 13 - Debrief: explain the count before writing the letters
- Scenario A: G3 T1 P0 A1 L1.
- Scenario B: G2 T0 P1 A0 L2.
- Scenario C: G3 T2 P0 A1 L2.
- The transferable rule is more valuable than any single answer.

> NOTES: Talk track: Have students defend each component. Common error: using parity as a synonym for number of children. [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 14 - Break: return ready to date, assess, and trend
- Keep the evidence ladder and GTPAL rules visible.
- After break, we calculate EDC/EDB and connect it to the initial prenatal visit.

> NOTES: Talk track: Collect one unresolved documentation question. Common error: treating dating as separate from risk assessment. [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 15 - Naegele's rule is a starting estimate
- Formula: first day of LMP minus 3 months, plus 7 days, plus 1 year.
- Example: LMP 10 March 2026 -> EDB 17 December 2026.
- The estimate assumes a regular 28-day cycle and ovulation around day 14.

> NOTES: Talk track: Work the date slowly and have students check the calendar. Common error: changing the year incorrectly. [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 16 - Ultrasound dating can supersede an uncertain calendar
- Uncertain LMP, irregular cycles, lactation, or recent hormonal contraception reduce calendar reliability.
- First-trimester crown-rump length is the most accurate dating method.
- Redating thresholds follow current departmental guidance.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask: "What information would make you distrust the LMP?" Common error: treating Naegele's rule as more precise than the data allow. [ACOG 2017] [Lowdermilk] [VERIFY: department redating thresholds]

## Slide 17 - Initial prenatal care starts with a whole-person history
- Medical, surgical, obstetric, gynecologic, family, and genetic history.
- Medications, allergies, substances, mental health, IPV, and safety.
- Language, housing, food, transportation, and insurance access.
- The baseline establishes what later trends can mean.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask which questions require privacy. Common error: completing only the biomedical history. [ACOG 2017] [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 18 - Baseline labs create comparison points
| Assessment | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| blood type/Rh and antibody screen | alloimmunization risk |
| CBC | anemia and platelet baseline |
| rubella, HBsAg, HIV, syphilis | infection and prevention planning |
| GC/CT, urinalysis/culture | infection and urinary risk |
| cervical cytology when due | preventive care |

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask: "Which result changes later prophylaxis?" Common error: treating the baseline panel as a one-time checklist without follow-up. [ACOG 2017] [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 19 - Risk stratification changes follow-up intensity
- Baseline findings determine referral and visit frequency.
- Possible referrals include maternal-fetal medicine, nutrition, social work, and behavioral health.
- The nurse closes the loop so referral does not become abandonment.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask students to identify a barrier that could prevent a referral from being completed. Common error: documenting referral without confirming access. [ACOG 2017] [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 20 - Maternal adaptation is a predictable pattern with safety exceptions
| System | Expected adaptation | Safety connection |
|---|---|---|
| cardiovascular | plasma volume and cardiac output rise | dilutional anemia; trend BP |
| respiratory | tidal volume rises | assess dyspnea context |
| GI | motility slows | reflux and constipation |
| GU | GFR rises | bacteriuria still matters |
| psychological | ambivalence to acceptance/attachment | assess support and distress |

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask: "Which adaptation is expected but still needs context?" Common error: labeling every symptom as normal pregnancy. [Lowdermilk] [Silbert-Flagg]

## Slide 21 - Position changes can correct supine hypotension
- After the first trimester, supine positioning can reduce venous return.
- A left lateral tilt supports circulation.
- Link the intervention to the mechanism, not a memorized position alone.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask students to explain why the position works. Common error: using a position without assessing symptoms and gestational context. [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 22 - Psychological adaptation is individual and nonlinear
- Ambivalence, acceptance, and attachment may overlap.
- Partners and siblings adapt on their own timelines.
- Assess support, stress, safety, and mental health without assuming a single sequence.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask: "What question invites rather than assumes attachment?" Common error: treating a textbook stage as a required emotional performance. [Silbert-Flagg] [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 23 - Active learning 2: date first, then decide what data to trust
- Station 1: calculate EDB from a reliable LMP.
- Station 2: calculate from an irregular-cycle history.
- Station 3: choose when ultrasound dating should be recommended.
- Explain the clinical reason, not only the arithmetic.

> NOTES: Talk track: Rotate groups every 4 minutes. Common error: providing a precise date from unreliable input. [ACOG 2017] [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 24 - Screening the history protects vulnerable patients
- Ask about IPV, depression, food insecurity, housing, immigration-related access barriers, and language preference.
- Use a qualified interpreter when needed.
- Document the patient's needs and the response plan.
- A missed social cue can become a missed clinical cue.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask: "Which question would you ask privately?" Common error: using a family member as interpreter for sensitive information. [ACOG 2017] [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 25 - First action: a positive hCG is not the end of assessment
Scenario: A patient reports amenorrhea, nausea, and a positive hCG. She has unilateral pelvic pain and dizziness.

A. Reassure her that nausea confirms a normal pregnancy.
B. Assess stability and escalate for possible ectopic emergency.
C. Schedule routine prenatal care in four weeks.
D. Calculate GTPAL before taking vital signs.

**Answer: B.** Stability and red-flag assessment come before routine sequencing.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask students to name the missing vital signs and bleeding assessment. Common error: allowing a probable sign to suppress an urgent cue. [ACOG PB 200] [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 26 - The other three options fail because they ignore cue priority
- A fails: symptoms do not prove location or viability.
- C fails: routine timing is unsafe when urgent cues are present.
- D fails: documentation supports care but cannot replace immediate stability assessment.
- Transferable principle: classify evidence, then prioritize safety.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask which cue changed the plan. Common error: letting the requested task dictate the first action. [ACOG PB 200] [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 27 - Evaluation starts at the first visit
- Compare later BP, weight, fundal height, symptoms, and laboratory trends with baseline.
- Reconcile discrepancies rather than averaging them away.
- The EHR should make change visible across visits.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask: "What does a trend add that a single value cannot?" Common error: reading each visit in isolation. [ACOG 2017] [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 28 - Documentation must preserve the patient's story and the clinical meaning
- Record the gestational history in a reproducible structure.
- State the source of dating information and its reliability.
- Separate reported symptoms from examiner findings.
- Document teaching, patient response, and follow-up plan.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask students to rewrite a vague note into a data-rich one. Common error: charting "pregnant" without the evidence source. [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 29 - A five-step response pattern handles mixed cues
- Recognize: what did the patient report or what did you observe?
- Analyze: what can and cannot this finding prove?
- Prioritize: is there an immediate safety cue?
- Take action: what assessment, referral, or teaching comes next?
- Evaluate: what trend will show whether the plan worked?

> NOTES: Talk track: Apply the sequence to the ectopic scenario and to uncertain dating. Common error: skipping analysis and jumping from cue to intervention. [Lowdermilk] [Dickison et al. 2019]

## Slide 30 - Exit ticket checks classification and calculation
- Calculate one EDB from a supplied LMP.
- Classify three findings as presumptive, probable, or positive.
- State what twins do to G and L.
- Name one reason a first-trimester ultrasound may supersede Naegele's estimate.

> NOTES: Talk track: Collect for remediation flags. Common error: writing "positive hCG" as a positive sign of pregnancy. [Lowdermilk] [ACOG 2017]
