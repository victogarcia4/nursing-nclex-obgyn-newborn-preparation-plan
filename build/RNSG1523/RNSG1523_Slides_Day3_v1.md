# RNSG 1523 Nursing I - Day 3 Slide Deck
## Ongoing Prenatal Care, Screening, and Diagnostics

> WO-C | 140 minutes | Markdown slide deck | Version v1

## Slide 1 - A prenatal trend is more informative than an isolated value
- Day 3: visit schedule, screening tests, diagnostic tests, and abnormal-result response.
- Clinical judgment thread: analyze cues, generate solutions, and evaluate outcomes.

> NOTES: Talk track: Open with a serial BP trend. Ask: "Which value is the cue, and what does the trend add?" Common error: waiting for one dramatic value. [ACOG 2017] [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 2 - These three objectives define today's work
1. Outline the prenatal visit schedule and assessments at each visit.
2. Distinguish screening from diagnostic tests, with correct timing.
3. Interpret common abnormal prenatal laboratory and diagnostic findings and identify the nursing action.

> NOTES: Talk track: Read the objectives verbatim. Ask students to predict which test is easiest to misclassify. Common error: treating a screen as a diagnosis. [ACOG PB 226] [ACOG CO 797] [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 4 - The hook: classify before you counsel
- Poll: cfDNA is a screen or a diagnosis?
- Poll: a positive GBS culture requires antepartum antibiotics or intrapartum prophylaxis?
- Poll: a fundal-height discrepancy is a single-value problem or a trend problem?

> NOTES: Talk track: Record the reasoning. Common error: relying on test names rather than purpose and timing. [ACOG PB 226] [ACOG CO 797] [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 5 - Prenatal visits are scheduled surveillance opportunities
- Typical schedule: every 4 weeks to 28 weeks.
- Then every 2 weeks from 28-36 weeks.
- Then weekly from 36 weeks to birth.
- Risk status can change the schedule.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask: "What would make this schedule insufficient?" Common error: treating the schedule as fixed even when risk changes. [ACOG 2017] [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 6 - Every visit creates a comparable data point
- Blood pressure, weight, urine protein/glucose, fundal height, and FHR.
- Edema, fetal movement report, symptoms, and warning-sign review.
- Compare with prior visits, not only with a reference range.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask students to name one trend that would change escalation. Common error: documenting normal today without reviewing change over time. [ACOG 2017] [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 7 - Fundal height is a screening trend, not a diagnosis
- Between approximately 20 and 36 weeks, centimeters roughly track gestational weeks.
- A discrepancy greater than 2-3 cm warrants evaluation.
- Consider dates, fetal growth, fluid, fibroids, and technique.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask: "What data would you gather before naming the cause?" Common error: calling FGR from fundal height alone. [Lowdermilk] [ACOG PB 227]

## Slide 8 - EHR trending turns routine care into early recognition
- Graph BP, weight, fundal height, and symptoms across visits.
- Use structured fields so the change is visible to the care team.
- Close the loop on abnormal results and referrals.
- Informatics is part of safety, not clerical work.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask students to identify one handoff risk when a result is not tracked. Common error: assuming the ordering clinician will notice every trend. [ACOG 2017] [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 9 - Screening estimates probability; diagnosis establishes certainty
| Feature | Screening | Diagnostic testing |
|---|---|---|
| Goal | estimate risk | establish or exclude a condition |
| Result | probability | more definitive finding |
| Next step | counseling and options | management discussion |
| Patient choice | informed choice | informed consent and choice |

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask students to complete the sentence: "A positive screen means..." Common error: telling a patient a positive screen confirms disease. [ACOG PB 226]

## Slide 10 - Screening windows matter as much as test names
| Test | Typical timing | Main purpose |
|---|---|---|
| cfDNA/NIPT | at least 9-10 weeks | aneuploidy risk screening |
| quad screen | 15-22 weeks | NTD and aneuploidy risk |
| GCT -> OGTT | 24-28 weeks | gestational diabetes screening |
| GBS culture | 36 0/7-37 6/7 weeks | early-onset neonatal GBS risk |

> NOTES: Talk track: Place each test on a class timeline. Common error: treating a test's timing as optional. [ACOG PB 226] [ACOG PB 190] [ACOG CO 797]

## Slide 11 - cfDNA is powerful screening, not diagnostic confirmation
- cfDNA screens for trisomy 21, 18, 13, and sex-chromosome conditions.
- A positive result requires genetic counseling and diagnostic confirmation.
- Discuss limitations and patient values before testing.
- SMFM Consult Series #74 may layer onto PB 226; verify current guidance.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask: "What is the nurse's first response to a positive cfDNA result?" Common error: using the screening result as a fetal diagnosis. [ACOG PB 226] [VERIFY: SMFM Consult Series #74 current guidance]

## Slide 12 - Quad-screen results require context before action
- Increased AFP may reflect an NTD, abdominal-wall defect, multiples, or incorrect dates.
- Decreased AFP can alter aneuploidy risk.
- Follow-up commonly includes ultrasound and counseling.
- Do not interpret a marker in isolation.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask: "Which alternative explanation must be checked first?" Common error: translating one abnormal marker directly into one diagnosis. [ACOG PB 226] [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 13 - GBS screening changes intrapartum planning
- Culture at 36 0/7-37 6/7 weeks.
- Positive result -> intrapartum antibiotic prophylaxis, not routine antepartum treatment.
- Penicillin G is first-line when appropriate.
- Document result visibility and handoff.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask: "Why does timing of prophylaxis matter?" Common error: treating colonization during pregnancy as an antepartum infection in every patient. [ACOG CO 797]

## Slide 14 - Diabetes screening guidance carries a currency flag
- ACOG's 2024 Clinical Practice Update supersedes parts of PB 190.
- The two-step approach is documented in the lesson plan.
- Thresholds used for teaching must match current departmental guidance.
- Flag uncertainty rather than silently selecting a threshold.

> NOTES: Talk track: Explain how students should handle conflicting textbook and policy values. Common error: treating a legacy table as current without verification. [ACOG PB 190] [ACOG 2024] [VERIFY: department thresholds]

## Slide 15 - Rh(D) prevention depends on status and exposure
| Cue | Nursing implication |
|---|---|
| Rh-negative, antibody-negative | prophylaxis at approximately 28 weeks per protocol |
| Rh-negative with bleeding | assess need for dose per protocol |
| invasive procedure | assess need for dose per protocol |
| birth of Rh-positive newborn | postpartum dose within protocol window |

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask: "What information must be confirmed before administration?" Common error: giving a dose without checking antibody status or the exposure. [ACOG PB 181] [VERIFY: departmental dosing protocol]

## Slide 16 - A positive screen ends with a counseling conversation
- Explain that probability changed; certainty did not necessarily follow.
- Offer referral and diagnostic options according to the clinical plan.
- Support acceptance or decline after informed counseling.
- Document questions, decision, and follow-up.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask students to practice one neutral result explanation. Common error: using alarm language before confirmatory information exists. [ACOG PB 226]

## Slide 17 - CVS and amniocentesis answer diagnostic questions
| Test | Typical timing | Sample/purpose | Key nursing role |
|---|---|---|---|
| CVS | 10-13 weeks | placental tissue; genetic diagnosis | consent, Rh assessment, postprocedure teaching |
| amniocentesis | 15+ weeks for genetics | amniotic fluid; genetic diagnosis | consent, FHR/contraction monitoring |

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask: "What does informed consent require beyond a signature?" Common error: describing a diagnostic test as routine screening. [ACOG PB 226] [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 18 - Testing is an informed choice regardless of age or baseline risk
- Offer both screening and diagnostic options according to current guidance.
- Explain benefits, limitations, possible results, and follow-up.
- Respect refusal without reducing care quality.
- Use interpreters and accessible materials.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask: "What is advocacy when the patient declines?" Common error: equating counseling with persuasion. [ACOG PB 226]

## Slide 19 - Ultrasound answers several different clinical questions
- Dating and viability.
- Anatomy and placental location.
- Growth and fluid assessment.
- The nursing preparation depends on the study and gestational age.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask students to connect the clinical question to the ultrasound type. Common error: assuming every ultrasound has the same purpose. [Lowdermilk] [ACOG PB 226]

## Slide 20 - NST and BPP assess fetal well-being through different data
- NST evaluates fetal heart-rate response.
- BPP combines multiple biophysical observations.
- A nonreactive or concerning result triggers the next clinical decision, not an automatic diagnosis.
- Follow the departmental escalation pathway.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask: "What information is missing before deciding on escalation?" Common error: treating one test result as the entire fetal assessment. [Lowdermilk] [VERIFY: department escalation pathway]

## Slide 21 - Active learning 1: serial visits reveal the changing hypothesis
- BP trend: 118/72 -> 132/84 -> 146/94.
- New 2+ urine protein appears at the third visit.
- Students identify the trend, analyze the cue cluster, and prioritize follow-up.
- No single value tells the whole story.

> NOTES: Talk track: Give groups 5 minutes to state the next nursing action and the data they need. Common error: focusing only on the last number. [ACOG PB 222] [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 22 - Debrief: the trend changes what the nurse does next
- Recheck and assess symptoms according to protocol.
- Escalate the pattern and document the prior values.
- Ask about headache, visual change, RUQ/epigastric pain, and other warning signs.
- Do not wait for a single confirmatory label before responding to danger cues.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask students to give an SBAR opening sentence. Common error: reporting only the current BP without the trend. [ACOG PB 222] [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 23 - A test timeline prevents timing errors
| Gestational window | Examples |
|---|---|
| 9-13 6/7 weeks | cfDNA; first-trimester screening; CVS |
| 15-22 weeks | quad screen; amniocentesis at or after 15 weeks |
| 24-28 weeks | GCT/OGTT; repeat CBC and Rh assessment |
| 36 0/7-37 6/7 weeks | GBS culture |

> NOTES: Talk track: Have students place a new test on the timeline and state its purpose. Common error: memorizing week ranges without linking them to the test question. [ACOG PB 226] [ACOG CO 797] [ACOG PB 190]

## Slide 24 - Result tracking is a team responsibility
- Verify that abnormal results are visible and acknowledged.
- Use closed-loop communication for referrals and diagnostic follow-up.
- Document patient notification and understanding.
- Escalate when follow-up is delayed or inaccessible.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask: "What is the safety risk of a result sitting unread in a portal?" Common error: assuming result release equals result follow-up. [ACOG 2017] [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 25 - First action: a positive screen must be framed accurately
Scenario: A cfDNA report is high risk for trisomy 21. The patient asks, "Does my fetus have Down syndrome?"

A. Confirm the diagnosis from the screen.
B. Explain that this is a screening result and arrange counseling/diagnostic follow-up.
C. Tell the patient to ignore the result until delivery.
D. Recommend termination without further discussion.

**Answer: B.** It preserves accuracy, autonomy, and the next clinical step.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask students to phrase the first two sentences to the patient. Common error: replacing probability with certainty. [ACOG PB 226]

## Slide 26 - The other three options fail because they collapse choice or certainty
- A fails: a screen does not establish a diagnosis.
- C fails: follow-up is needed to clarify risk and options.
- D fails: counseling must be nondirective and patient-centered.
- Transferable principle: explain the result, then support informed next steps.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask which option violates both clinical and ethical reasoning. Common error: confusing urgency for follow-up with permission to decide for the patient. [ACOG PB 226]

## Slide 27 - Diagnostic procedures require preparation and recovery teaching
- Confirm consent and the planned procedure.
- Assess Rh(D) status when indicated.
- Monitor FHR, bleeding, pain, and contractions after the procedure.
- Give clear return precautions and a contact pathway.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask students to name what they would document after amniocentesis. Common error: teaching only the procedure and not the postprocedure warning signs. [ACOG PB 226] [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 28 - Abnormality is a cue to investigate, not a conclusion
- Trend the result and compare with gestational age.
- Check specimen, dates, symptoms, and related findings.
- Use the appropriate confirmatory pathway.
- Keep the patient informed while uncertainty remains.

> NOTES: Talk track: Apply this sequence to elevated AFP and a fundal-height discrepancy. Common error: anchoring on the first plausible diagnosis. [ACOG PB 226] [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 29 - The clinical judgment sequence protects against anchoring
- Recognize the abnormal or changing cue.
- Analyze context, timing, and linked findings.
- Prioritize immediate safety.
- Generate the next assessment or referral.
- Evaluate whether follow-up occurred.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask students to map the cfDNA scenario onto the sequence. Common error: stopping after recognizing the abnormal result. [Dickison et al. 2019] [ACOG PB 226]

## Slide 30 - Active learning 2: sort tests by purpose and timing
- Sort eight tests into screening or diagnostic.
- Place each on the gestational timeline.
- Add the nursing response to an abnormal result.
- Defend one classification that initially seemed ambiguous.

> NOTES: Talk track: Debrief with a class timeline. Common error: sorting by invasiveness instead of purpose. [ACOG PB 226] [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 31 - Exit ticket checks timing and Rh prevention
- At 28 weeks, an Rh-negative, antibody-negative client asks what is given and why.
- Name two other exposures that can trigger Rh(D) immune globulin assessment.
- State the difference between a positive screen and a diagnosis.

> NOTES: Talk track: Collect for remediation. Common error: giving antepartum antibiotics for a positive GBS culture. [ACOG PB 181] [ACOG CO 797] [ACOG PB 226]

## Slide 32 - Screening results need a patient-centered explanation
- Name what the test can and cannot establish.
- Explain the next diagnostic or follow-up option.
- Support the patient's decision without pressure.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask students to rewrite a frightening result statement into an accurate one. Common error: converting risk information into certainty. [ACOG PB 226]

## Slide 33 - Rh prevention is a protocol plus a handoff
- Confirm Rh and antibody status.
- Identify pregnancy events that require dose assessment.
- Document the indication and ensure follow-up is visible.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask students to identify two events besides birth. Common error: treating Rh(D) immune globulin as a one-time prenatal task. [ACOG PB 181]

## Slide 34 - Trend review is the outcome-evaluation step
- Compare the current value with prior visits.
- Review whether referral, testing, and counseling occurred.
- Escalate missed follow-up or a worsening pattern.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask what outcome would show the plan was effective. Common error: ending the plan when the test is ordered. [ACOG 2017] [Lowdermilk]
