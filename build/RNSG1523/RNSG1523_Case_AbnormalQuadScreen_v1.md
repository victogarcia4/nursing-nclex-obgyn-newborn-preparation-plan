# Unfolding Case (WO-G) — Abnormal Quad Screen Counseling
**Course:** RNSG 1523 Nursing I · **Traces to objectives:** 1523-D3-2, 1523-D3-3, 1523-D3-4 · **Format:** 6 scenes, one per NCJMM operation.

**Patient:** Priya, 33, G2 P1, at 17 weeks. Quad screen drawn at 16 weeks returns **screen positive for trisomy 21**.

---

### Scene 1 — Recognize Cues (highlight)
**Chart:** Quad screen at 16 weeks: screen positive for trisomy 21. Patient calls, anxious, asking "Does my baby have Down syndrome?" No prior diagnostic testing. Anatomy ultrasound pending.
**Item.** Highlight the cue that most shapes the nurse's teaching priority.
**Key:** the phrase "screen positive" (a risk estimate, not a diagnosis) and the patient's assumption that it is a diagnosis.
**Rationale.** The teaching gap is screening-versus-diagnostic understanding [ACOG PB 226].

### Scene 2 — Analyze Cues (matrix)
**Item.** For each test, mark *Screening* vs *Diagnostic*.

| Test | Answer |
|---|---|
| Quad screen | Screening |
| Cell-free DNA (NIPT) | Screening |
| Chorionic villus sampling | Diagnostic |
| Amniocentesis | Diagnostic |

**Rationale.** Only CVS and amniocentesis provide a karyotype; the quad screen and cfDNA estimate risk [ACOG PB 226].

### Scene 3 — Prioritize Hypotheses (cloze/dropdown)
**Item.** The nurse explains that a positive quad screen means **[an increased risk estimate]**, and the next step that can provide a definitive answer is **[diagnostic testing such as amniocentesis]** if the patient chooses it.
**Rationale.** Prioritize correcting the screening/diagnostic distinction before any decision [ACOG PB 226].

### Scene 4 — Generate Solutions (extended multiple response)
**Item.** Select the 3 appropriate counseling actions.
- ✅ Explain that the screen estimates risk and does not diagnose
- ✅ Describe diagnostic options (amniocentesis) and that they are the patient's choice
- ✅ Offer genetic counseling referral
- ❌ Tell her the baby has Down syndrome
- ❌ Recommend a specific pregnancy decision
- ❌ State that no further information is available

**Rationale.** Nondirective, accurate information plus a genetic-counseling referral supports informed autonomy [ACOG PB 226].

### Scene 5 — Take Actions (SATA)
**Item.** Which nursing actions support ethical, patient-centered care? Select all that apply.
- ✅ Provide balanced written information on screening vs diagnostic testing
- ✅ Support her right to accept or decline diagnostic testing
- ✅ Document teaching and referral
- ❌ Schedule an amniocentesis without discussing risks and consent

**Rationale.** Informed consent means she may decline; the nurse informs and supports rather than steers [ACOG PB 226].

### Scene 6 — Evaluate Outcomes (trend)
**Follow-up:** Visit 1 — patient verbalizes understanding that the screen is a risk estimate; Visit 2 — meets genetic counselor, elects amniocentesis; Visit 3 — receives and understands the diagnostic result and her options.
**Item.** Which interpretation best evaluates the counseling?
**Key:** The patient now correctly distinguishes screening from diagnosis and is making an informed, autonomous choice — the teaching objective was met.
**Rationale.** Evaluate counseling by the patient's accurate understanding and autonomous decision-making, not by which choice she makes [ACOG PB 226].

---

## Debrief — three decision points students most often miss
1. **Letting "positive" imply "diagnosis."** A screen positive is a probability; saying otherwise causes harm.
2. **Being directive.** The nurse informs and refers; the pregnancy decision belongs to the patient.
3. **Skipping consent for the diagnostic test.** CVS/amniocentesis are elective and carry risks that require informed consent.
