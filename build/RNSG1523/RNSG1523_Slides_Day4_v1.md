# RNSG 1523 Nursing I - Day 4 Slide Deck
## Nutrition, Medications, Discomforts, Breast Care, and Warning Signs

> WO-C | 140 minutes | Markdown slide deck | Version v1

## Slide 1 - Normal discomforts and warning signs require different actions
- Day 4: nutrition, activity, medication risk, comfort, breast care, and triage.
- Clinical judgment thread: prioritize hypotheses, generate solutions, and evaluate outcomes.

> NOTES: Talk track: Open with a triage card. Ask: "What makes this a warning sign rather than a normal discomfort?" Common error: using reassurance before assessment. [Lowdermilk] [ACOG CO 804]

## Slide 2 - These four objectives define today's work
1. Plan prenatal nutrition and physical activity individualized by BMI and trimester.
2. Interpret pregnancy medication risk using legacy FDA letters and current PLLR narrative labeling.
3. Teach evidence-based relief for common discomforts of pregnancy.
4. Distinguish normal discomforts from warning signs requiring immediate evaluation.

> NOTES: Talk track: Read the objectives verbatim. Ask students to mark the objective that most affects patient teaching. Common error: treating a medication label as a substitute for clinical judgment. [IOM/NRC 2009] [FDA PLLR 2014] [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 4 - The hook: sort the cue before you name the diagnosis
- Heartburn after a meal.
- Persistent vomiting with weight loss and ketonuria.
- Mild dependent edema late in the day.
- Headache with visual change and epigastric pain.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask students to sort normal discomfort, needs follow-up, or urgent evaluation. Common error: using symptom familiarity to dismiss a dangerous cluster. [ACOG PB 189] [ACOG PB 222] [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 5 - Energy needs rise later, not at the same rate throughout pregnancy
- No routine additional calories are needed in the first trimester.
- Approximate additions: 340 kcal/day in the second trimester and 452 kcal/day in the third.
- Individualize for activity, fetal number, health, and access.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask: "Why is a blanket calorie prescription unsafe?" Common error: doubling calories or ignoring food insecurity. [IOM/NRC 2009] [ACOG CO 548] [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 6 - Prepregnancy BMI sets the singleton weight-gain range
| BMI | Category | Total singleton gain |
|---|---|---|
| <18.5 | underweight | 28-40 lb |
| 18.5-24.9 | normal weight | 25-35 lb |
| 25.0-29.9 | overweight | 15-25 lb |
| >=30.0 | obesity | 11-20 lb |

> NOTES: Talk track: Have students calculate which range applies to a sample patient. Common error: using current pregnancy weight instead of prepregnancy BMI. [IOM/NRC 2009] [ACOG CO 548]

## Slide 7 - Food safety teaching should explain the hazard
- Avoid unpasteurized dairy and deli meats unless heated.
- Avoid high-mercury fish, raw/undercooked foods, and raw sprouts.
- Limit caffeine to less than 200 mg/day in the lesson-plan teaching frame.
- No known safe amount of alcohol exists during pregnancy.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask students to connect each food to its risk. Common error: giving a list without checking cultural food practices or access. [ACOG CO 548] [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 8 - Nutrition plans must fit culture and resources
- Ask about food practices, fasting, pica, and food insecurity.
- Connect eligible patients with WIC, SNAP, food pantries, or community health workers.
- Offer language-appropriate materials.
- Preserve dignity: access is a clinical variable, not a moral judgment.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask: "What would you do if the recommended food is unaffordable?" Common error: prescribing nutrition without assessing resources. [IOM/NRC 2009] [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 9 - Activity is generally beneficial when contraindications are absent
- Exercise can support health during pregnancy.
- Modify for overheating, dehydration, contact sports, fall risk, scuba diving, and prolonged supine positioning.
- Ask about baseline activity and obstetric conditions.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask: "What assessment comes before advising vigorous activity?" Common error: either prohibiting activity broadly or ignoring risk cues. [ACOG CO 804]

## Slide 10 - Stop-exercise symptoms are escalation cues
- Vaginal bleeding or fluid leakage.
- Regular painful contractions.
- Dyspnea before exertion, dizziness, or chest pain.
- Calf pain/swelling or weakness affecting balance.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask students to choose the first action when a patient reports one of these during exercise. Common error: advising the patient to push through symptoms. [ACOG CO 804]

## Slide 11 - Legacy letters are a crosswalk, not current PLLR labeling
| Legacy term | Historical teaching meaning |
|---|---|
| A/B/C/D/X | letter categories used in older labels and texts |
| Current practice | narrative risk-benefit information |
| Nursing action | reconcile medication, assess risk, coordinate with prescriber |

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask: "Why do students still encounter the letters?" Common error: treating the letters as the current FDA labeling system. [FDA PLLR 2014] [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 12 - PLLR gives the clinician a narrative risk frame
- Section 8.1: Pregnancy.
- Section 8.2: Lactation.
- Section 8.3: Females and males of reproductive potential.
- Risk summary, data, and clinical considerations replace A/B/C/D/X letters.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask students which section they would use for a patient planning pregnancy. Common error: searching for a single letter instead of reading the risk summary. [FDA PLLR 2014]

## Slide 13 - Medication reconciliation is the first nursing safeguard
- Review prescription, over-the-counter, herbal, and recreational substances.
- Identify potential teratogens and untreated maternal disease.
- Do not tell a patient to stop chronic therapy independently.
- Coordinate with the prescriber and document the plan.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask: "What is the harm of abrupt self-discontinuation?" Common error: assuming stopping is automatically safer than continuing. [FDA PLLR 2014] [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 14 - Teratogen teaching requires a current source and a plan
- Examples requiring careful review include isotretinoin, valproic acid, methotrexate, ACE inhibitors/ARBs, warfarin, and live vaccines.
- The nurse identifies the exposure and escalates to the prescriber.
- The patient receives a coordinated risk-benefit explanation.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask students to avoid telling the patient to make an independent medication change. Common error: naming a risk without giving a safe next step. [FDA PLLR 2014] [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 15 - Nausea relief starts with low-risk measures
- Small frequent meals and a dry carbohydrate before rising.
- Avoid triggers; consider ginger.
- Vitamin B6 with or without doxylamine is a first-line pharmacologic frame.
- Persistent vomiting, weight loss, dehydration, or ketonuria changes the hypothesis.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask: "Which cue moves this from common discomfort to hyperemesis concern?" Common error: calling all vomiting morning sickness. [ACOG PB 189] [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 16 - Common discomforts have mechanism-linked relief
| Discomfort | Mechanism | Relief |
|---|---|---|
| reflux | LES relaxation and slowing | small meals, upright after eating |
| constipation | slowed motility/pressure | fiber, fluids, activity |
| backache | lordosis/strain | posture, support, side-lying |
| edema/varicosities | venous compression | elevation, side-lying, support |
| urinary frequency | pressure and increased GFR | Kegels, fluids earlier in day |

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask students to add one red flag to each row. Common error: restricting total fluids for urinary frequency. [ACOG PB 189] [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 17 - Supine hypotension is corrected by changing position
- Vena caval compression can reduce venous return.
- Use a left lateral position or tilt when symptomatic.
- Reassess symptoms and maternal response.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask students to explain the mechanism. Common error: treating positioning as a ritual without reassessment. [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 18 - Breast care prepares for lactation without unsafe routines
- Normal changes include enlargement, tenderness, darkened areolae, and colostrum.
- Use a supportive, well-fitted bra.
- Avoid soap on nipples; routine nipple toughening is not needed.
- Refer for lactation support and assess work-related pumping needs.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask: "Which teaching is outdated or unnecessary?" Common error: recommending nipple stimulation without considering contraction risk. [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 19 - Warning signs need a call-now or come-in-now plan
- Vaginal bleeding or leaking fluid.
- Decreased/absent fetal movement.
- Severe headache, visual disturbance, RUQ/epigastric pain, sudden edema.
- Fever, dysuria/flank pain, regular contractions before 37 weeks.
- Persistent vomiting or self-harm thoughts.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask students to pair each cue with an action. Common error: telling the patient to wait for the next appointment. [ACOG CO 804] [ACOG PB 222] [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 20 - The preeclampsia warning cluster outranks routine discomfort
- Headache + visual change + epigastric/RUQ pain + sudden edema is an emergency cue cluster.
- Proteinuria is not required when severe features are present.
- Assess and escalate; do not reassure first.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask: "Which cue makes this more than a headache?" Common error: waiting for a urine result before responding. [ACOG PB 222] [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 21 - Active learning 1: triage cards require urgency ranking
- Sort ten findings into normal discomfort or warning sign.
- Rank warning signs by urgency.
- Name the first nursing action and the data to collect.
- Defend the ranking with the cue cluster.

> NOTES: Talk track: Run a timed sort, then ask teams to defend one disagreement. Common error: ranking by how familiar a symptom feels. [ACOG PB 222] [ACOG CO 804] [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 22 - Debrief: the patient's statement is itself a cue
- "Something is wrong" warrants listening and focused assessment.
- Ask what changed, when it started, and what makes it better or worse.
- Validate the concern while determining urgency.
- Escalate when the pattern exceeds routine teaching.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask students to practice a validating opening. Common error: dismissing an intuitive concern because one vital sign is normal. [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 23 - A two-column comparison prevents false reassurance
| Likely common discomfort | Needs immediate evaluation |
|---|---|
| mild reflux after meals | severe persistent headache |
| dependent edema late in day | sudden face/hand edema with symptoms |
| urinary frequency without pain | dysuria with fever/flank pain |
| occasional backache | regular painful contractions before 37 weeks |
| mild nausea | vomiting with dehydration/ketonuria |

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask students to state what additional cue would move a finding across columns. Common error: assuming the symptom name determines urgency. [ACOG PB 189] [ACOG PB 222] [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 24 - First action: medication reconciliation at seven weeks
Scenario: A patient taking lisinopril for chronic hypertension is 7 weeks pregnant.

A. Tell the patient to stop it immediately without contacting anyone.
B. Contact the prescriber promptly and coordinate a pregnancy-safe plan.
C. Tell the patient to continue it and avoid discussing risk.
D. Delay action until the next routine prenatal visit.

**Answer: B.** Reconciliation and coordinated risk-benefit planning protect both patient and fetus.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask which data are needed: dose, indication, BP, other medications, and access. Common error: replacing coordination with a unilateral stop/continue instruction. [FDA PLLR 2014] [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 25 - The other three medication options fail in predictable ways
- A fails: abrupt changes can harm the patient and do not create a plan.
- C fails: the exposure requires prompt review, not silence.
- D fails: delay may prolong an avoidable exposure.
- Transferable principle: identify the risk and close the coordination loop.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask students to write an SBAR message to the prescriber. Common error: treating a medication label as a self-management instruction. [FDA PLLR 2014]

## Slide 26 - Active learning 2: build the medication case bowtie
- Condition: pregnancy with chronic medication exposure.
- Actions: reconcile/notify prescriber; assess maternal status and teaching needs.
- Monitor: BP and medication response/adverse effects.
- Exclude independent discontinuation and false reassurance.

> NOTES: Talk track: Give pairs 6 minutes. Common error: selecting "stop medication" as a universal action. [FDA PLLR 2014] [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 27 - A triage handoff should contain data, not a conclusion
- Identify gestational age and onset.
- State the symptom, severity, trend, and associated cues.
- Give vital signs and relevant history.
- Ask for the next action and document the response.

> NOTES: Talk track: Model an SBAR opening for headache with visual changes. Common error: saying "the patient is fine" or "the patient has preeclampsia" before reporting data. [ACOG PB 222] [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 28 - Unit synthesis: prevention and triage are linked
- Nutrition and activity reduce avoidable risk when individualized.
- Medication reconciliation catches exposure before harm escalates.
- Comfort teaching includes a safety net.
- Warning-sign teaching gives the patient permission to seek care.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask students to connect one comfort measure to one escalation cue. Common error: separating teaching from emergency preparedness. [Lowdermilk] [ACOG CO 804]

## Slide 29 - Exit ticket checks action and urgency
- Name three warning signs and the specific action for each.
- State the current PLLR sections.
- Give one relief measure for reflux and one for constipation.
- State one reason to escalate persistent vomiting.

> NOTES: Talk track: Collect for remediation flags. Common error: naming a symptom without an action. [FDA PLLR 2014] [ACOG PB 189] [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 32 - A teach-back reveals whether the safety net is usable
- Ask the patient to state which symptom needs immediate care.
- Ask where and how they will seek help.
- Correct gaps without shaming.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask students to model a teach-back prompt. Common error: asking "Do you understand?" instead of testing action readiness. [Lowdermilk] [ACOG CO 804]

## Slide 33 - Risk labeling must include the untreated condition
- Medication risk is not assessed by exposure alone.
- Untreated hypertension, seizure disorder, or other disease can also harm pregnancy.
- Coordinate prescriber review rather than issuing independent stop orders.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask students to identify the risk of abrupt discontinuation. Common error: assuming all medication discontinuation is safer. [FDA PLLR 2014] [Lowdermilk]

## Slide 34 - Comfort teaching includes a clear escalation boundary
- Give one safe measure for the symptom.
- Name the cue that changes the action.
- State the call-now or come-in-now route.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask students to use nausea and headache as examples. Common error: offering relief without a safety-net instruction. [ACOG PB 189] [ACOG PB 222]

## Slide 35 - Exam synthesis: match the cue to the action
- Normal discomfort with no red flags -> teach relief and follow-up.
- Persistent or worsening symptoms -> focused assessment.
- Severe-feature cluster or emergency cue -> immediate escalation.

> NOTES: Talk track: Ask students to create one cue/action pair for each row. Common error: using reassurance as the action for every common symptom. [ACOG PB 189] [ACOG PB 222]
