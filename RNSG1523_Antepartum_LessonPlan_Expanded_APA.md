# DAY 1 (Monday, Week 1) — Reproductive Foundations & Preconception

**Topics:** brief reproductive anatomy and physiology (A&P) · life-cycle issues · dysmenorrhea · menopause · contraceptives · genetics and the pre-pregnancy examination

### Learning objectives
By the end of the session, the student will be able to:
1. Review female reproductive A&P and the phases of the menstrual cycle.
2. Differentiate primary from secondary dysmenorrhea and describe nursing management of each.
3. Describe the physiologic changes, risk profile, and health-promotion priorities of menopause.
4. Compare contraceptive methods by mechanism, typical-use effectiveness, contraindications, and patient teaching.
5. Explain the components of preconception counseling and prenatal genetic carrier screening.

### 1A. Reproductive A&P review (brief) — *Physiologic Integrity; Lifespan*
- Ovarian cycle: follicular → ovulation → luteal.
- Uterine (endometrial) cycle: menstrual → proliferative → secretory.
- Hormonal axis: GnRH → FSH/LH → estradiol/progesterone; the LH surge triggers ovulation.
- **Teaching device:** draw the two cycles on one timeline so students see that "day 14" is an *average*, not a rule.
> *Source:* Lowdermilk et al. (2024); Silbert-Flagg & Pillitteri (2023).

### 1B. Life-cycle issues — *Lifespan thread*
Menarche → reproductive years → perimenopause → menopause → postmenopause. Frame every later topic against where the patient sits on this arc.
> *Source:* Lowdermilk et al. (2024).

### 1C. Dysmenorrhea — *Physiologic Integrity; Pharmacology; Teaching*
| | Primary | Secondary |
|---|---|---|
| Pathology | None — prostaglandin-mediated myometrial contraction | Identifiable pelvic pathology |
| Typical onset | 6–12 months after menarche | Later; new or worsening pain |
| Timing | Begins with/just before flow, 8–72 h | May precede menses by days; may be constant |
| Common causes | — | Endometriosis (leading cause in adolescents), fibroids, adenomyosis, PID |

- **Recognize cues:** pain pattern, relation to menses, school/work absence, response to prior therapy.
- **Take action:** NSAIDs started at or just before pain onset (inhibit prostaglandin synthesis); hormonal suppression (COC, LNG-IUD, DMPA); heat; exercise.
- **Evaluate/refer:** ACOG advises investigating secondary causes when there is no clinical improvement within **3–6 months** of appropriate therapy.
- **Vulnerable populations:** adolescents often normalize severe pain — ask directly.
> *Sources:* American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists [ACOG] (2018a); Lowdermilk et al. (2024).

### 1D. Menopause — *Physiologic + Psychological Integrity; Health Promotion; Ethics*
- **Definition:** 12 consecutive months of amenorrhea without another cause.
- **Recognize cues:** VMS (hot flashes, night sweats), GSM (vaginal dryness, dyspareunia, urinary urgency), sleep disruption, mood changes.
- **Risk profile:** accelerated bone loss/osteoporosis, adverse lipid shift, increased CHD risk.
- **Health promotion:** calcium and vitamin D, weight-bearing and resistance exercise, smoking cessation, DXA screening per guideline, sleep hygiene.
- **Hormone therapy — the risk/benefit conversation:** HT remains the most effective treatment for VMS and GSM and prevents bone loss and fracture. The benefit–risk ratio is **favorable** for symptomatic women **under age 60 or within 10 years of menopause onset** with no contraindications; it becomes **less favorable** when initiated more than 10 years out or after age 60, because of greater absolute risks of CHD, stroke, and VTE. Individualize; reevaluate periodically.
- **Ethics/Legal + Patient-Centered Care:** shared decision-making; the nurse's role is accurate information and advocacy, not persuasion.
> *Sources:* The North American Menopause Society [NAMS] 2022 Hormone Therapy Position Statement Advisory Panel (2022); Lowdermilk et al. (2024).

### 1E. Contraception — *Pharmacology; Cultural/Economic; Ethics; Teaching*

| Category | Examples | Mechanism | Key teaching / red flags |
|---|---|---|---|
| Behavioral | FAM/FABM, withdrawal, LAM | Timing / suppression of ovulation | Highest typical-use failure; often the best cultural/religious fit — respect it and teach it well |
| Barrier | Male & female condom, diaphragm, cervical cap, sponge, vaginal pH modulator | Physical/chemical block | **Condoms are the only method that also reduces STI transmission** |
| Combined hormonal | COC, transdermal patch, vaginal ring | Ovulation suppression + cervical mucus | VTE risk; **contraindicated in smokers ≥ 35 y**, migraine with aura, uncontrolled hypertension. Teach **ACHES** |
| Progestin-only | POP, DMPA, etonogestrel implant | Cervical mucus, endometrium, ± ovulation | POP requires strict same-time dosing; DMPA → bone density considerations, delayed return to fertility |
| LARC | LNG-IUD, Cu-IUD, implant | Local + systemic | Most effective reversible methods; teach **PATCH**; check strings |
| Permanent | Tubal ligation/salpingectomy, vasectomy | Occlusion | Informed consent, permanence, documented capacity |
| Emergency | LNG, UPA, Cu-IUD | Delays/inhibits ovulation (Cu-IUD also post-fertilization) | Sooner is better; Cu-IUD most effective EC |

- **Clinical tool to name in class:** the **U.S. MEC** — CDC's evidence-based eligibility categories (1 = no restriction → 4 = unacceptable health risk) for using a given method with a given condition. The 2024 edition replaced the 2016 edition and added chronic kidney disease, revised recommendations for breastfeeding/postpartum, obesity, VTE, thrombophilia, SLE, sickle cell disease, and others, and added newer formulations including the vaginal pH modulator.
- **Ethics/Vulnerable Populations — say this out loud:** the U.S. MEC explicitly frames contraceptive counseling against the history of forced sterilization and reproductive coercion affecting racial and ethnic minority groups, people with disabilities, and other marginalized groups. Person-centered, non-directive counseling is an ethical obligation, not a style preference.
- **Economic access:** cost, insurance, Title X clinics, pharmacy access.
> *Sources:* Nguyen et al. (2024); Centers for Disease Control and Prevention [CDC] (2024); ACOG (2017); Lowdermilk et al. (2024).

### 1F. Preconception care and genetics — *Health Promotion; Informatics; Vulnerable Populations*
- **Folic acid:** the USPSTF gives an **A recommendation** that all persons planning or capable of pregnancy take a daily supplement containing **0.4–0.8 mg (400–800 mcg)** of folic acid. Emphasize: NTDs form before most people know they are pregnant — this is a *preconception* intervention.
- **Immunizations:** live vaccines (measles-mumps-rubella, varicella) before conception.
- **Chronic disease optimization:** diabetes glycemic control, hypertension, thyroid, epilepsy — coordinate with the prescriber.
- **Medication review:** identify teratogens *before* conception (see Day 4).
- **Substance use:** tobacco, alcohol, opioids, cannabis — screen and refer, do not lecture.
- **Genetic screening:** family history/pedigree, ethnicity-informed or expanded carrier screening (CF, SCD, Tay-Sachs, spinal muscular atrophy), AMA counseling.
- **Informatics:** genetic databases, pedigree software, EHR problem lists.
> *Sources:* U.S. Preventive Services Task Force [USPSTF] (2023); ACOG (2017); ACOG (2020c).

### Active learning
- **AL1 — Contraceptive matching (Analyze/Generate):** three personas — (a) 16-year-old wanting confidentiality; (b) 38-year-old who smokes 10 cigarettes/day; (c) 6-weeks-postpartum, exclusively breastfeeding. Students select and justify a method, citing U.S. MEC logic.
- **AL2 — NGN bowtie:** adolescent with worsening dysmenorrhea unresponsive to NSAIDs × 6 months. Actions to take / condition most likely / parameters to monitor.

**Exit ticket:** List two absolute contraindications to estrogen-containing contraception and expand the mnemonic ACHES.

# DAY 2 (Tuesday, Week 1) — Confirming and Dating Pregnancy · OB History

**Topics:** presumptive/probable/positive signs · GTPAL · EDC/EDB and Naegele's rule · initial prenatal visit · maternal physiologic and psychological adaptation

### Learning objectives
1. Classify presumptive, probable, and positive signs of pregnancy.
2. Document an obstetric history accurately using GTPAL.
3. Calculate an EDC/EDB using Naegele's rule and state its limitations.
4. Describe the components of the initial prenatal visit, including baseline laboratory studies.
5. Summarize normal maternal physiologic and psychological adaptations of the first trimester.

### 2A. Signs of pregnancy — *Recognize Cues*
| Class | Who perceives it | Examples | Why it isn't diagnostic |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Presumptive** (subjective) | The pregnant person | Amenorrhea, nausea/vomiting, fatigue, breast tenderness, urinary frequency, quickening | All have non-pregnancy causes |
| **Probable** (objective) | The examiner | Goodell sign (cervical softening), Chadwick sign (bluish cervix/vagina), Hegar sign (softening of lower uterine segment), ballottement, Braxton Hicks contractions, positive hCG, uterine enlargement | Tumors and hormonal states can mimic |
| **Positive** (diagnostic) | The examiner | FHR by Doppler/auscultation, fetus visualized on ultrasound, fetal movement palpated by examiner | Attributable **only** to a fetus |

**Teaching device:** "Who felt it, and could anything else explain it?" resolves nearly every exam item in this category.
> *Source:* Lowdermilk et al. (2024); Silbert-Flagg & Pillitteri (2023).

### 2B. GTPAL — *Informatics/Documentation; Take Action*
- **G**ravida — total pregnancies including the current one (a twin pregnancy is **one** gravida).
- **T**erm — births ≥ 37 0/7 wk.
- **P**reterm — births 20 0/7–36 6/7 wk.
- **A**bortions — losses < 20 wk, spontaneous or induced.
- **L**iving — children currently alive (twins = 2).
- **Rule to drill:** *G counts pregnancies; L counts children.* Multiples split the two.
- **Practice set:** (i) currently pregnant, one term birth, one miscarriage at 9 wk → G3 T1 P0 A1 L1. (ii) currently pregnant, twins born at 35 wk both living → G2 T0 P1 A0 L2.
> *Source:* Lowdermilk et al. (2024).

### 2C. EDC/EDB — Naegele's rule — *Take Action; Informatics*
- **Formula:** first day of LMP − 3 months + 7 days (+ 1 year).
- **Worked example:** LMP 10 March 2026 → 10 December 2025 + 7 days → **17 December 2026**.
- **Limitations:** assumes a regular 28-day cycle with ovulation on day 14. Irregular cycles, recent hormonal contraception, lactation, or uncertain LMP → **first-trimester ultrasound (crown–rump length) is the most accurate dating method**; discuss redating criteria with the department's textbook thresholds.
> *Sources:* ACOG (2017, reaffirmed guidance on dating); Lowdermilk et al. (2024).

### 2D. Initial prenatal visit — *Assessment; Vulnerable Populations; Safety*
- Comprehensive history: medical, surgical, obstetric, gynecologic, family, genetic, psychosocial, medication, substance use.
- Physical: full exam including pelvic; baseline vital signs, height, weight, **BMI** (drives Day 4's weight-gain targets).
- **Baseline labs:** blood type and Rh with antibody screen, CBC, rubella immunity, HBsAg, HIV, syphilis, gonorrhea/chlamydia, urinalysis and culture, cervical cytology per screening interval.
- **Screening beyond labs:** IPV, depression, food insecurity, housing, immigration-related access barriers, language preference.
- **Risk stratification:** determines visit frequency and referral (maternal-fetal medicine, nutrition, social work).
> *Sources:* ACOG (2017); Lowdermilk et al. (2024).

### 2E. Maternal adaptation (brief) — *Physiologic + Psychological Integrity*
- **Cardiovascular:** ↑ plasma volume > ↑ red cell mass → physiologic (dilutional) anemia; ↑ cardiac output; supine hypotensive syndrome after the first trimester → **left lateral tilt**.
- **Respiratory:** ↑ tidal volume, mild dyspnea, nasal congestion.
- **GI:** progesterone-mediated slowing → reflux, constipation.
- **GU:** ↑ glomerular filtration rate, urinary frequency, ureteral dilation → asymptomatic bacteriuria matters.
- **Psychological:** ambivalence → acceptance → attachment; Rubin's maternal tasks; the partner's and siblings' adaptation.
> *Sources:* Lowdermilk et al. (2024); Silbert-Flagg & Pillitteri (2023).

### Active learning
- **AL1 — GTPAL rapid-fire:** five scenarios, whiteboards, 60 seconds each.
- **AL2 — Dating station:** three Naegele calculations, then a case where the LMP is uncertain → students decide to recommend ultrasound dating and explain why.

**Exit ticket:** Calculate one EDC and classify three given findings as presumptive/probable/positive.

# DAY 3 (Monday, Week 2) — Ongoing Prenatal Care · Screening & Diagnostics

**Topics:** prenatal visit schedule and per-visit assessments · screening tests · diagnostic tests · responding to abnormal values

### Learning objectives
1. Outline the prenatal visit schedule and the assessments performed at each visit.
2. Distinguish screening from diagnostic tests, with correct timing for each.
3. Interpret common abnormal prenatal laboratory and diagnostic findings and identify the appropriate nursing action.

### 3A. Visit schedule and per-visit assessment — *Recognize Cues; Safety/QI*
- **Typical schedule:** every 4 weeks to 28 weeks → every 2 weeks from 28–36 weeks → weekly from 36 weeks to birth (modified by risk).
- **Every visit:** blood pressure, weight, urine dipstick (protein, glucose), fundal height, FHR, edema assessment, fetal movement report, review of danger signs.
- **Fundal height:** McDonald measurement in centimeters approximates gestational weeks between roughly 20 and 36 weeks; a discrepancy > 2–3 cm warrants evaluation.
- **QI/Informatics:** trending BP, weight, and fundal height *across* visits in the EHR is what makes deterioration visible — a single value rarely is.
> *Sources:* ACOG (2017); Lowdermilk et al. (2024).

### 3B. Screening tests — *Analyze Cues; Take Action*

| Test | Timing | Screens for | Nursing response to abnormal |
|---|---|---|---|
| First-trimester screen (NT + PAPP-A + hCG) | 10 3/7–13 6/7 wk | Trisomy 21, 18 | Offer genetic counseling + diagnostic testing |
| **cfDNA / NIPT** | ≥ 9–10 wk | Trisomy 21, 18, 13; sex chromosomes | **Screening only** — a positive result requires diagnostic confirmation |
| Quad screen (AFP, hCG, uE3, inhibin A) | 15–22 wk | NTD, trisomy 21, 18 | ↑ AFP → NTD, abdominal wall defect, multiples, or wrong dates → ultrasound. ↓ AFP → aneuploidy risk |
| 50 g GCT → 100 g 3-h OGTT if elevated | 24–28 wk | GDM | Two-step approach; ACOG's 2024 Clinical Practice Update supersedes parts of Practice Bulletin 190 — verify the department's current threshold |
| **GBS vaginal–rectal culture** | **36 0/7–37 6/7 wk** | Neonatal GBS early-onset disease | Positive → intrapartum antibiotic prophylaxis (penicillin G first-line) in labor, **not** in the antepartum period |
| Blood type, Rh, antibody screen | Initial + 28 wk | Rh alloimmunization | Rh-negative, antibody-negative → **Rh(D) immune globulin at ~28 wk** and within 72 h of birth if the newborn is Rh-positive; also after CVS, amniocentesis, bleeding, trauma, pregnancy loss |
| CBC / Hgb-Hct | Initial + ~24–28 wk | Anemia | Iron supplementation, dietary teaching, recheck |

**Key conceptual point to hammer:** *screening changes probability; diagnosis changes certainty.* Every positive screen ends with a counseling conversation, not a conclusion.
> *Sources:* ACOG (2020c) [PB 226]; ACOG (2020a) [CO 797]; ACOG (2018b) [PB 190]; ACOG (2024) [Clinical Practice Update on diabetes screening]; ACOG (2017) [PB 181, Rh D]; Nguyen et al. (2024) for contraception-adjacent counseling ethics.

### 3C. Diagnostic tests — *Ethics/Legal; Advocacy*
| Test | Timing | Purpose | Nursing role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ultrasound | Any trimester | Dating, viability, anatomy, placental location, growth | Prepare, position (left tilt after 1st trimester), full bladder for early transabdominal |
| **CVS** | 10–13 wk | Fetal karyotype/CMA from placental tissue | Informed consent; Rh(D) immune globulin if Rh-negative; post-procedure bleeding/cramping precautions |
| **Amniocentesis** | ≥ 15 wk (genetic) | Karyotype/CMA; later, fetal lung maturity or infection | Consent; bladder empty; Rh(D) immune globulin if Rh-negative; monitor FHR and contractions after |
| NST | 3rd trimester | Fetal well-being | Reactive vs non-reactive; left lateral position |
| BPP | 3rd trimester | Composite well-being score | Understand the 5 components and scoring |
| MCA Doppler | As indicated | Fetal anemia surveillance | Referral-level test |

**Ethics/Legal core teaching:** ACOG's position is that **all patients should be offered both screening and diagnostic testing regardless of age or baseline risk**, and every patient has the right to accept or decline after counseling. Testing is an informed choice, not a default. The nurse supports the decision the patient makes.
> *Sources:* ACOG (2020c); Lowdermilk et al. (2024).

### Active learning
- **AL1 — Unfolding case (Recognize → Analyze):** serial prenatal visits with BP rising 118/72 → 132/84 → 146/94 and new 2+ proteinuria. Students identify the *trend* as the cue, not any single value, and prioritize.
- **AL2 — Sorting activity:** eight tests sorted into screening vs diagnostic, with correct gestational-age windows placed on a timeline.

**Exit ticket:** An Rh-negative, antibody-negative client at 28 weeks — what is given, why, and what else triggers a dose?

# DAY 4 (Tuesday, Week 2) — Nutrition · Medications · Discomforts · Breast Care · Warning Signs

**Topics:** nutrition and activity · medication risk classification (legacy categories and PLLR) · common discomforts and relief · breast care · warning signs · unit synthesis

### Learning objectives
1. Plan prenatal nutrition and physical activity individualized by BMI and trimester.
2. Interpret pregnancy medication risk using both the legacy FDA letter categories and the current PLLR narrative labeling.
3. Teach evidence-based relief measures for common discomforts of pregnancy.
4. Distinguish normal discomforts from warning signs requiring immediate evaluation.

### 4A. Nutrition — *Nutrition/Dietary Modifications; Cultural; Economic*
- **Energy:** approximately **+340 kcal/day in the second trimester** and **+452 kcal/day in the third**; no increase in the first trimester.
- **Folic acid:** 400–800 mcg/day preconception and early pregnancy (USPSTF **A** recommendation); prenatal vitamins typically supply 600 mcg DFE in pregnancy.
- **Iron, calcium, protein, iodine, omega-3s:** per the department's textbook values.
- **Gestational weight gain by prepregnancy BMI (IOM/NRC 2009 — still the operative U.S. guideline):**

| Prepregnancy BMI | Category | Total gain (singleton) |
|---|---|---|
| < 18.5 | Underweight | 28–40 lb (12.5–18 kg) |
| 18.5–24.9 | Normal weight | 25–35 lb (11.5–16 kg) |
| 25.0–29.9 | Overweight | 15–25 lb (7–11.5 kg) |
| ≥ 30.0 | Obesity | 11–20 lb (5–9 kg) |

- **Twin gestation (IOM, provisional):** normal weight 37–54 lb; overweight 31–50 lb; obesity 25–42 lb.
- **Food safety — teach the *why*, not just the list:** avoid unpasteurized dairy and deli meats unless heated (*Listeria monocytogenes*), high-mercury fish (shark, swordfish, king mackerel, tilefish), raw/undercooked meat, eggs, and sprouts. Caffeine generally limited to < 200 mg/day. **Alcohol: no known safe amount at any point.**
- **Cultural/Economic:** food practices, religious fasting, pica, food insecurity → **WIC**, SNAP, food pantry, community health worker referral. Offer Spanish-language materials where indicated.
> *Sources:* Institute of Medicine & National Research Council [IOM & NRC] (2009); ACOG (2013/reaffirmed); USPSTF (2023); Lowdermilk et al. (2024).

### 4B. Physical activity — *Health Promotion; Safety*
- In the absence of obstetric or medical contraindications, **exercise in pregnancy is safe and desirable**; those already active may continue, including vigorous aerobic activity.
- Observed benefits include reduced GDM, reduced cesarean and operative vaginal birth, faster postpartum recovery, and a role in preventing postpartum depressive disorders.
- **Modify:** avoid supine positions for prolonged periods after the first trimester, avoid overheating and dehydration, avoid contact sports, activities with high fall risk, and scuba diving.
- **Stop and call:** vaginal bleeding, regular painful contractions, fluid leakage, dyspnea before exertion, dizziness, chest pain, calf pain/swelling, muscle weakness affecting balance.
> *Source:* ACOG (2020b) [CO 804].

### 4C. Medication risk classification — *Pharmacology; Ethics; Safety*

**Legacy FDA pregnancy categories (A, B, C, D, X):**
| Category | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **A** | Adequate, well-controlled human studies show no fetal risk in the first trimester |
| **B** | Animal studies show no risk, no adequate human studies; or animal risk not confirmed in humans |
| **C** | Animal studies show adverse effects; no adequate human studies; potential benefit may justify risk |
| **D** | Positive evidence of human fetal risk; benefit may still outweigh risk in serious conditions |
| **X** | Studies show fetal abnormalities; risk clearly outweighs any benefit — **contraindicated** |

**What replaced them — the PLLR:**
- FDA published the **Pregnancy and Lactation Labeling Rule** on **December 4, 2014**, effective **June 30, 2015**.
- The rule **removes the A/B/C/D/X letter categories** from all human prescription drug and biological product labeling.
- It replaces them with three narrative subsections: **8.1 Pregnancy** (including any pregnancy exposure registry), **8.2 Lactation**, and **8.3 Females and Males of Reproductive Potential** — each containing a risk summary, supporting data, and clinical considerations.
- Labels must be updated when information becomes outdated.
- **Why nursing students still learn the letters:** they persist in textbooks, in older references, and in NCLEX-style item stems. Teach the letters as *legacy vocabulary* and the PLLR as *current practice*.

**Named teratogens worth memorizing (former category X or D):** isotretinoin, thalidomide, warfarin, valproic acid, methotrexate, ACE inhibitors and ARBs, live vaccines, tetracyclines, androgens.

**Take Action:** medication reconciliation at the first prenatal visit; never advise a patient to stop a chronic medication independently — coordinate with the prescriber; the harm of untreated maternal disease is part of the risk calculation.
> *Sources:* U.S. Food and Drug Administration [FDA] (2014); Content and Format of Labeling for Human Prescription Drug and Biological Products (2014); Lowdermilk et al. (2024).

### 4D. Discomforts of pregnancy and relief — *Comfort/Positioning; Teaching*
| Discomfort | Physiologic basis | Nursing relief measures |
|---|---|---|
| Nausea and vomiting | hCG, estrogen, gastric slowing | Small frequent meals, dry carbohydrate before rising, avoid triggers, **ginger**, **vitamin B6 (pyridoxine) ± doxylamine** as first-line pharmacotherapy |
| Heartburn/reflux | Progesterone-mediated LES relaxation | Small meals, remain upright 1–2 h after eating, avoid triggers, antacids per provider |
| Constipation / hemorrhoids | Slowed motility, iron, pressure | Fiber, fluids, activity, stool softeners per provider, sitz baths |
| Backache / round ligament pain | Lordosis, ligament stretching | Posture, pelvic tilt, supportive footwear, side-lying with pillow support, heat |
| Dependent edema / varicosities | Venous compression | Elevate legs, side-lying, avoid crossing legs and constrictive clothing, support hose |
| Supine hypotensive syndrome | Vena caval compression | **Left lateral position/tilt** — teach this as a reflex |
| Leg cramps | Multifactorial | Dorsiflex the foot with the knee extended; stretching |
| Urinary frequency | Uterine pressure, ↑ GFR | Kegels, fluids earlier in day — **do not** restrict total fluids |
| Fatigue / insomnia | Hormonal, mechanical | Rest periods, sleep hygiene, side-lying with pillows |

**Escalation point:** persistent vomiting with weight loss, dehydration, or ketonuria is not "morning sickness" — evaluate for hyperemesis gravidarum.
> *Sources:* ACOG (2018c) [PB 189]; Lowdermilk et al. (2024).

### 4E. Breast care — *Health Promotion; Teaching*
- Normal changes: enlargement, tenderness, darkened areolae, prominent Montgomery tubercles, colostrum in late pregnancy.
- Supportive, well-fitted bra (day and, if needed, night); avoid soap on nipples; no routine nipple "toughening."
- **Safety point:** nipple stimulation releases oxytocin and can provoke uterine contractions — relevant in preterm risk.
- Breastfeeding preparation: prenatal education, flat/inverted nipple assessment, lactation consultant referral, workplace pumping rights.
> *Source:* Lowdermilk et al. (2024).

### 4F. ⚠ Warning signs of pregnancy — *Emergency/Critical Thinking; Advocacy; Safety*
Teach as a single memorized "call now / come in now" list:
- Vaginal bleeding
- Leaking of fluid (suspected rupture of membranes)
- **Decreased or absent fetal movement**
- Severe or persistent headache
- Visual disturbances — blurring, spots, scotomata
- Epigastric or right upper quadrant pain
- Sudden facial or hand edema
- Persistent vomiting; inability to keep fluids down
- Fever ≥ 38 °C (100.4 °F) or chills
- Dysuria, flank pain
- Regular uterine contractions before 37 weeks
- Any thoughts of self-harm or harming the baby

**Cluster to name explicitly:** headache + visual changes + epigastric pain + sudden edema = **preeclampsia with severe features until proven otherwise** — this is the single highest-yield pattern of the unit.
**Advocacy/Communication:** teach patients that being dismissed is not a reason to go home. Teach students that "the patient says something is wrong" is a cue.
> *Sources:* ACOG (2020a, 2020b); Lowdermilk et al. (2024).

### Active learning
- **AL1 — Triage cards (Analyze/Prioritize):** ten findings; sort "normal discomfort" vs "warning sign," then rank the warning signs by urgency.
- **AL2 — NGN medication case (Take Action):** a client with chronic hypertension on lisinopril presents at 7 weeks pregnant. Students identify the problem, the correct action (contact the prescriber — do not simply stop or continue), and the teaching.
- **Wrap-up:** unit concept map on the board + exam blueprint walkthrough.

**Exit ticket:** Name three warning signs and the specific action for each.

## Reference-quality notes for faculty
1. **ACOG documents are corporate-authored.** In APA 7, the organization is both author and, for the Committee Opinion/Practice Bulletin series, effectively the publisher. Some departments prefer the ACOG in-house citation style (e.g., "Prevention of group B streptococcal early-onset disease in newborns. ACOG Committee Opinion No. 797. *Obstet Gynecol* 2020;135:e51–72."). Confirm which style the department wants on student handouts.
2. **Currency check before you teach.** Two items in this list have known successors in progress: (a) ACOG's 2024 Clinical Practice Update on diabetes screening updates portions of Practice Bulletin 190; (b) ACOG endorsed SMFM Consult Series #74 on cfDNA screening in late 2025, which layers onto Practice Bulletin 226. Verify both on acog.org the week before Day 3.
3. **Textbook editions.** The two textbooks listed are the standard U.S. maternal-newborn nursing texts. **Replace them with whichever edition the department has adopted** — page-level alignment with the exam matters more than recency here.
4. **The IOM 2009 gestational weight gain report remains the operative U.S. guideline** despite its age; ACOG continues to reference it. Do not substitute a newer-looking but non-authoritative source.
5. **What was deliberately excluded:** UpToDate (subscription tertiary source, not appropriate as a primary citation for students), commercial NCLEX prep publishers, Wikipedia, nursing blogs, Osmosis/Level Up RN and similar video channels, and any secondary site restating ACOG content.
